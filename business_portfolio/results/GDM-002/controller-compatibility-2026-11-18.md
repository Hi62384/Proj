## Game-owned controller integration

An optional `controller_bridge.gd` forwards movement requests to a game-owned controller without moving the body or owning navigation. A game-provided physical-arrival predicate is checked before an acknowledgment; stale or changed requests are rejected. The bridge withdraws obsolete work on supersession, cancellation, coordinator removal or bridge removal. Tests exercise false and nonboolean predicates, modified or duplicate events, reentrant cancellation, reconnection, recovery and pending-request restoration. The bridge cannot prove that a game's predicate is truthful or manage game-specific movement on its own.

An original example CharacterBody3D controller completed a one-passenger collision-platform transfer through the bridge. In a separate clean consumer project, tests on each engine recreated the game-owned character during boarding, then separately recreated that character and the first platform mid-ride; both scenarios completed the two-platform transfer with no occupied seat left. The second platform and rest of the scene remained in memory. These are synthetic examples, not integration with an existing external game, a multiplayer test or a general game-world save system.

A new regression initially found that the example controller could emit arrival while still airborne at the target horizontal coordinates. It was corrected to wait until grounded; the airborne regression and controller tests passed on each engine.

## Compatibility and visual evidence

On this Windows x86-64 machine, the full 45-script baseline, install/reinstall check and headless editor marker check passed on Godot 4.4.1 (`49a5bc7b6`), 4.5.1 (`f62fdbde1`) and 4.7.2 (`ed1daf0bf`). The baseline source fingerprint was `12bb93666047101f186cc4f7b4a0de407e149352a3a67b1980a10e3364319498`. After that full run, two restore fixtures were added and the compatibility smoke selection changed; a per-file comparison found no changes to add-on, demo or example-controller source. Both added tests passed separately on all three engines. A final-source 14-test smoke, install/reinstall and editor-marker check also passed on each, with stable source fingerprint `444ff8bd3b674f2a82356596f09b03649c4393cef0f74362fff075fd44d0b281`. The complete 47-script suite then passed against staged snapshot `18j` on each of the three engines.

The free 4.4.1 and 4.7.2 archives came from Godot's official download pages ([4.4.1](https://godotengine.org/download/archive/4.4.1-stable/), [4.7.2](https://godotengine.org/download/archive/4.7.2-stable/)) and were checked against official release checksums before extraction. The first 4.7.2 extraction attempt rejected the verified archive because its executable exceeded an overly conservative extracted-size limit; the limit was corrected after inspecting the archive, and extraction succeeded. No operating-system installation or redistribution occurred. Offline Godot runs continued to emit a sandbox root-certificate-store warning.

Real Godot renderer captures at 1152×648 were inspected on the three versions using the NVIDIA OpenGL compatibility renderer. Programmatically emitted button signals passed pause/resume, save/load and service-toggle checks. An initial HUD probe showed 17 waiting while three passengers were boarding; the display had omitted boarding states even though the ledger was correct. A new regression initially failed, then passed after grouping boarding as waiting, alighting as riding, and cancellations separately. A corrected rendered capture showed 19 waiting and one cancelled. This does not establish interactive mouse/keyboard behavior, accessibility, DPI scaling, other graphics drivers or the editor gizmo's appearance.
