Automated online income portfolio strategy

Build a small portfolio of useful products around original digital assets and reusable validation software. First test a small original asset pack, with a strict craft and demand gate. Build an offline localization checker next. Explore a supplier-catalogue validation subscription later, after a paid business pilot proves repeat use.

Prepared for a Canada-based solo creator. Research checked 13 October 2026. All planning amounts are CAD unless a source explicitly uses USD or EUR. This is a research and implementation plan: nothing has been purchased, subscribed to, publicly launched or sent to prospective customers.

The practical conclusion

  1. Sell a narrow result before building a platform. A small original asset pack is the fastest proposed paid test if the craft gate passes. The next software test catches broken localization placeholders, missing keys and malformed markup locally. Charge for a complete workflow, tests and convenience. Free assets, Weblate and custom scripts are real competitors.
  2. Use one brand and one operational backbone initially. Share checkout, delivery, documentation, analytics, support forms, release testing and bookkeeping. A second product should reach substantially the same customers or reuse substantial tested code.
  3. Separate recurring revenue from recurring work. A static asset catalogue can approach one hour per week after stabilization. A SaaS product usually needs several hours per week plus unpredictable incidents. An income target that requires constant support is inconsistent with the low-maintenance objective.
  4. Buy validation and reliability with capital. More budget should fund a better sample, test devices, limited customer research and cash reserves. It should not automatically trigger advertising, ten subscriptions or ten launches.
  5. Use explicit stopping rules. At most one new build and two active demand experiments. If qualified prospects do not pay, stop or revise the offer. An attractive spreadsheet forecast cannot override that evidence.

What the numbers really mean

Confidence in the earnings scenarios is low. No sales or customer interviews for these exact proposed products have been observed. The restrained base model reaches about CAD303/month pretax at month 12 and CAD604 at month 24, requiring 17.4 and 13.5 owner hours in those months. At CAD25/hour, month-12 economic profit is negative and the full two-year base case still has not repaid all owner time. Optimistic CAD4,024–4,470/month at month 24 requires materially better demand and about 35–42 hours/month. These are conditional arithmetic cases, not measured expected earnings.

Why test assets before the checker? The asset test can expose a skill/differentiation failure within 12 craft hours, while a reliable checker may need 45–90 setup hours. That speed advantage disappears if original art production is not a strength. Make five competitive original pieces within the time box, then seek real project fit and paid adoption. If that fails, stop the asset line and move the localization checker or production calculator forward; do not keep its modeled revenue. Software fit is more plausible from the available background, but standalone willingness to pay is still unproved.

Important findings that change the plan

  • Godot’s official Asset Store does not currently support paid asset uploads in its published submission guidance. Use an eligible external seller platform; treat future store commerce as optional upside. S10 S11
  • Generic Godot save systems already face many free and inexpensive paid rivals. A free MIT web-export checker also exists. Neither category is an obvious empty market. S16 S17
  • Stock and template distribution can be automated, but supply is enormous. itch.io displayed roughly 60,000 paid asset listings at research time. Distinctive utility, quality and distribution matter more than generating more files. S13
  • Platform terms materially change returns: current Notion documentation lists 8% plus US$0.40 and a non-US payout conversion fee; TPT introduced an exclusive-selling option in October 2026. Do not reuse old creator-fee summaries. S32 S40 S41
  • GitHub Pages is not a free storefront/SaaS host under its usage policy. Use commercial-compatible hosting. n8n is not unrestricted open-source software that can simply be white-labelled into a paid hosting business. S65 S66

Planning assumptions and limits

Assume access to an existing computer and internet, working familiarity with game/software development, no established audience, no paid advertising history, and no demonstrated professional art, sound-design, teaching or sales expertise. These are planning assumptions, not claims about abilities. Allow 15–20 hours per week for the first two months, then 8–12 during validation and expansion. At 5 hours per week, roughly triple the build calendar and run only one product. The long-term maintenance target is 3–6 hours per week for a small stabilized portfolio; growth work is additional.

“Net profit” here means revenue less refunds, fees, direct delivery costs, software, acquisition spend and other modeled cash expenses, before personal income tax and before paying the owner for time. Economic profit separately values owner time at an illustrative CAD25/hour. No budget pays for living costs. Provincial registration, professional advice, insurance or unusual hardware may require additional funds and must be checked before launch.

How to read the opportunity estimates

The matrix ranks eight criteria on a 1–5 scale, where 5 is most attractive: long-term net-profit potential 20%, success evidence and realistic feasibility 20%, fulfilment automation 15%, scalability 10%, low recurring workload 15%, cost efficiency 8%, validation speed 7%, repeatability 5%. The weighted score is the sum of each rating divided by five and multiplied by its weight. Cost efficiency includes setup labour as well as cash.

Anchors: a 5 for low workload means a stable catalogue with few exceptions; 1 means service/incident-heavy operations. A 5 for speed means a useful paid test within about a week; 1 means a long build or audience dependency. A 5 for evidence would require demonstrated repeat paying demand for the exact offer; none of the novel software ideas meets that standard. Category evidence and practical feasibility support scores around 3. A score of 4 is not an 80% success probability. Differences below five points should usually be treated as ties.

For comparison only, each database record includes a no-sales case and two illustrative month-12 operating run rates. Unit contribution = illustrative price × (1 − variable fraction) − per-unit cash cost. Monthly operating net = volume × contribution − fixed cost. The fraction is a planning allowance for channel fees, refunds and acquisition where relevant; per-unit cost covers delivery or additional variable expense. These simplified catalogue comparisons exclude startup sunk cost, owner labour and income tax and are not the detailed portfolio model. They should never be added together across 54 rows.

For subscriptions, volume means active monthly accounts. For one-time products, it means orders during the month. In stock/royalty rows the price means illustrative creator receipts. In ad rows the unit means one thousand eligible views/plays. An apparently profitable run rate assumes those units can be acquired; that is the central unvalidated assumption. First-revenue ranges begin when an offer is ready for prospective buyers, and many products may never sell.

Sensitivity: the original-asset lead assumes you can make commercially competitive original work. Reducing its evidence/feasibility score from 4 to 2 cuts its score by eight points. Reducing cost efficiency from 5 to 2 cuts another 4.8. It then falls from 85 to 72.2. A one-point reduction to all game/creative skill-dependent feasibility scores costs four points each and puts production calculators ahead of assets. If workload matters twice as much, downloadable calculators/assets generally strengthen, while hosted B2B products fall. If upside receives more weight, CSV/data products rise. The result is therefore a validation order, not a scientifically unique optimum.

D Optimal business portfolio

The recommended sequence

First: one original modular asset kit (O01), conditional on a craft test. Build a small original workshop/interior environment with consistent units, collisions and a runnable demonstration. This is the fastest proposed sale and the first launch in the financial model. Original art skill is not assumed: if producing five competitive pieces takes over 12 hours or reviewers do not prefer them to free alternatives, skip this line and bring the software test forward, or validate a production calculator (O06). Re-run the model for that changed portfolio. Do not commission an entire catalogue before sales.

Second: offline localization QA software (O02). It uses software skills, avoids storing customer source files, and can share a validator engine with later products. The initial product is a downloadable browser app/CLI, not a hosted translation platform. Its purpose is technical preflight; it does not promise linguistic correctness. Start research and fixtures early, but the conservative modeled launch is month 3 so setup and customer learning are not compressed into an unrealistic first week.

Third: supplier-catalogue CSV preflight (O04), only after a paid pilot. Reuse tested CSV parsing, schemas, reporting, local processing and release infrastructure. Target small wholesalers importing supplier catalogues monthly. The different buyer base reduces gaming-market concentration, but it also creates new acquisition work. Keep it a separate product page and support queue under the same operating system; create a separate brand only if research shows the buyer needs it.

A higher-scoring asset or template is not automatically the first launch. Scores measure a set of uncertain trade-offs. Launch order also considers the value of learning, reusable infrastructure and skill fit. The first three blueprints are a staged option set, not authorization to launch all three at once.

  • Keep at most three supported core products through month 12, plus tightly related variations. Ten asset SKUs can be one product line; do not count them as ten independent businesses.

Commerce and delivery event flow

  1. Customer chooses a product and sees price, currency, licence, compatibility, refund terms and whether billing recurs.
  2. Hosted checkout collects payment and, where its contract applies, handles transaction sales tax. The business never stores card details.
  3. A signed event reaches the webhook receiver. Verify the raw-body signature. Persist a provider/event ID and receipt timestamp before acknowledging.
  4. A queue maps the event to an order, product and entitlement. Use a unique event key so retries cannot double-count revenue or send duplicate licences.
  5. Downloads come from immutable versioned storage or native platform delivery. Subscription entitlements have explicit active, grace, cancelled and expired states.
  6. Refunds and chargebacks are recorded as linked adjustments, not by deleting the original sale. Different provider events are not all new revenue.
  7. A daily read-only reconciliation compares orders, invoices, refunds and payouts to the provider. Payouts settle receivables; they are not additional sales.
  8. A weekly digest reports exceptions and useful decisions. No message is sent merely to announce that a routine job succeeded.

  • Use separate development and production configurations, least-privilege credentials and a secret manager. Credentials never belong in downloaded products, public repositories, browser JavaScript or support logs.

Blueprint 2 Offline localization quality checker

Second product with reusable validation software

Fast first result, explicit local processing, useful game-specific examples and reports that explain each rule. The free alternative is substantial: Weblate already checks many defects. Sell tested convenience and maintenance, not the assertion that nobody else can validate strings.

Verified: Weblate's documented checks show the problem class and its pricing shows a paid localization market (S21–S22). Unverified: willingness to buy this standalone product. Before building beyond 45 hours, conduct five voluntary problem demos and seek three CAD49 purchases. At least two buyers should use it again within 30 days. Ask about their most recent broken string/import and current workaround; do not lead with feature ideas.

Offer a genuinely useful free single-file checker. Paid personal licence: CAD49 for the current version and 12 months of updates; the purchased version keeps working. Test a CAD99 small-team licence only after team demand appears. An optional renewal for future updates must be separately and clearly priced. No renewal or team upsell revenue is assumed in the portfolio model.

6. Platform and software

Use a small TypeScript codebase with one pure validation core. Papa Parse can handle CSV; Ajv can validate schemas. Use native JSON parsing. Add a reviewed ICU parser only when needed. Keep a dependency lockfile and licence inventory. Do not copy a GPL localization implementation into a proprietary package without understanding and complying with its licence. Browser-first delivery avoids code-signing and installation friction at launch.

A diagnostic contains stable rule ID, severity, location, evidence and suggested action. A Web Worker handles larger inputs without freezing the UI. The core is deterministic and has no network dependency; licence/update checks are separate and cannot block existing offline work.

Parse without losing leading zeros or changing source strings. 4. Normalize to records and validate structure. 5. Compare placeholder multisets with separate handling for named and positional forms. 6. Add supported markup parsing and conservative length warnings. 7. Render an escaped report with file/key links. 8. Add drag-and-drop and a source-locale selector. 9. Add CLI exit codes: success, failed checks, invalid input. 10. Package a local zip, sample files, licence notices and quick-start. 11. Test on the exact supported browsers/OSes. 12. Have three fresh users complete the sample without coaching before selling.

10. AI and automation

Use AI for draft tests, explanations and documentation, then check every claim against the actual rule. Production checks do not need an AI API. Generate changelog drafts from approved commits. A support assistant can retrieve known answers and draft replies; unresolved false positives, refunds, security reports and compatibility promises go to a human. Never upload customer strings into an AI tool without a specific approved data flow.

Keep releases immutable and attach SHA-256 hashes plus a version manifest. Do not host the storefront on GitHub Pages. Ship one supported browser baseline first; add native installers only when enough customers justify signing, notarization and multi-OS support costs.

Proposed CAD59/month for one business, a clearly bounded number of profiles/jobs and an initial 50,000-row/10 MB per-file limit subject to performance testing. Client: file picker → parse → explicit field mapping → validation → human review → new exported file. Core records preserve raw cell text alongside parsed values. Reverse-engineering an ambitious target

Using base contribution before fixed costs/labour, a $5,000 monthly pretax target from one line alone would need approximately 218 asset orders, 121 localization licences, or 99 CSV subscribers after illustrative fixed costs of $10/$20/$60 respectively. This is arithmetic, not demand evidence. At 2% conversion, 121 licences need about 6,050 qualified visits/month, far beyond the base traffic cap. At 4% monthly churn, 99 subscribers require about four replacements each month before growth. Price, retention and distribution are more important than adding dozens of weak products.

Stress tests

With no sales anywhere, final cash is $0/$75/$350/$1,699 for initial budgets $0/$100/$500/$2,000. Cash lost is $0/$25/$150/$301, while owner time is still lost. A 30% itch withholding stress reduces available cash without reducing operating profit. A two-month payout-lag stress leaves about $1,208 of base M24 receipts outstanding versus $625 in the standard model. Tiny balances remain vulnerable to a single refund or frozen payout; expected fractional allowances do not remove that risk.

Tax is separate

A 30% reserve target on positive cumulative pretax profit is an illustrative cash policy, not the user's tax rate. Actual Canadian income tax, CPP, GST/HST and foreign withholding treatment require individual analysis. The model funds the reserve only from available cash and discloses unfunded targets. It does not model tax-payment dates, owner withdrawals or living costs. Cash left in a business is not automatically spendable take-home pay.

CASH TIMING, TAXES AND REINVESTMENT
Sales accrue as net receivables after refund and fee allowances. They cannot be spent in the sale month. The model pays them one model-month later only once each platform's threshold is met: itch US$5, Lemon Squeezy US$50. Localization and CSV share the Lemon Squeezy balance. Actual first payouts can take longer; a two-month-lag stress is supplied.

A one-time initial contribution is the only capital injection. Costs are paid from settled unreserved cash. If unavailable, optional tests/software/domain are skipped, rather than creating debt or secretly adding funding. The $0 plan uses a free storefront, existing equipment/connectivity and open-source tools; upgrades are contingent on received money. The $100 zero-sales stress skips localization QA because the inflation-adjusted charge exceeds cash available above its buffer. Skipped costs are enumerated in every path.

Tax reserve target is 30% of positive cumulative pretax profit, funded only as cash is available. It is shown separately from operating costs, with any unfunded target disclosed. It is not an estimate of the user's actual Canadian tax liability; no fiscal-year tax payments or loss-carryover rules are simulated. No owner drawings are modelled. For cautious planning, foreign withholding stress does not reduce this reserve as a tax credit; the withheld amount is separately tracked as an asset/prepayment pending actual tax advice.

The ordinary discretionary buffer is at least $50 or 15% of the prior month's gross sales, whichever is larger. It is an unspent cash target, not another expense or guaranteed protection. Expected fractional refunds/disputes smooth low-volume risk. A single full refund plus dispute fee after payout, frozen account or unexpected mandatory compliance cost can exceed a tiny seller's buffer. CAD0 incremental startup cost is not zero financial liability. Stop and re-budget if mandatory costs cannot be covered; never borrow based on this model.

Growth/reinvestment expense is QA + failed experiments + advertising, already included once in the P&L. It is not subtracted a second time. Scaled advertising is capped at 20% of prior settled receipts and $750/month before inflation, with available-cash constraints. Optional software has no automatic demand uplift. Larger budgets mainly buy tests and a buffer, so base-case month-12/24 revenue is identical across budgets while larger budgets have lower cumulative profit from higher tests.

ACCOUNTING IDENTITIES AND INDEPENDENT TESTS
Gross revenue - refunds - disputed revenue - fees - cash operating expenses = pretax profit.
Opening ordinary cash + settled receipts - cash operating expenses - tax-reserve transfer = closing ordinary cash.
Opening net receivables + current product contribution - settled receipts - withholding = closing net receivables.
Ordinary cash + tax-reserve cash + receivables + withholding = initial capital + cumulative pretax profit.
Economic profit = pretax profit - all owner hours x $25.

Calculations and checks

Net revenue excludes sales taxes collected for a tax authority. MRR includes active recurring subscriptions only; one-time download sales and annual cash receipts are not MRR. Churn = customers lost from the opening cohort ÷ opening active customers. CAC = attributable acquisition cash ÷ new paying customers; separately show fully loaded CAC including acquisition labour. LTV is not reliable with tiny cohorts: initially use realized 90-day contribution rather than price divided by a guessed churn rate.

Reconcile provider gross less refunds/tax/fees against payout movements and outstanding receivables. Maintain original currency plus actual CAD settlement conversion; do not convert historical cash using today's rate. A bundle gets one order and an explicit allocation, not a sale of every included item. Refunds link to the original order. Platform payouts are transfers, not new revenue. A tax reserve is restricted cash, not an operating expense or calculated tax liability.

Review rhythm and action rules

Daily automation checks should take no human attention when healthy. Spend 20–30 minutes weekly on exceptions, support patterns and acquisition evidence. Spend 45–60 minutes monthly reconciling cash, exporting backups, reviewing per-product hours and checking whether continuing each product still makes sense. Review fees/licences/platform policies quarterly and before any major expansion.

  • Scale candidate: two consecutive profitable months, repeatable acquisition, documented customer value, refund rate below 5% on a meaningful sample and owner-time return improving. Small samples need judgment.
  • Repair candidate: over 10% activation failure, any repeated paid-access failure, or support above 20 minutes/order. Diagnose before buying more traffic.
  • Retire candidate: no purchases after ten qualified demonstrations and two meaningful offer changes, or three months of negative contribution without a specific inexpensive fix. Insufficient exposure means “not tested,” not “failed.”
  • Do not automate: public statements, new spending commitments, significant refunds outside a preapproved policy, permanent customer-data deletion, security changes and legal judgments.

Intellectual property and truthful offers

All asset packs, audio, code, text and samples must be original or appropriately licensed for the exact redistribution. A licence to use an asset in a game often does not allow resale of the standalone file. Open source can require notices, source distribution or other obligations. AI output rights, training provenance, marketplace acceptance and copyright protection are distinct issues. Keep a manifest of authors, sources, licences and changes.

Do not commercialize Coraline characters, names, art, music, distinctive assets or other third-party fan-mod material without verified rights.
