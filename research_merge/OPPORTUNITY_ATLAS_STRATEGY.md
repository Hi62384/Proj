# Opportunity Atlas — niche-neutral research and action rules

**Scope:** 197 manually scoped sectors; **1,182** new niche/offer hypotheses; **114** preserved source records; **1,296 total records**. All CAD unless labelled otherwise. Snapshot 2026-10-13.

## Primary instruction: do NOT concentrate on gaming, Godot, AI, or any other sector based on past interests.

The search space is open across all lawful, plausible online business categories: trades, construction, healthcare administration, education, professional services, retail, logistics, hospitality, property, agriculture, manufacturing, events, creative assets, software, APIs, information services and many more. Never award preference points for personal existing projects without comparative data demonstrating superiority. **Research breadth and operational concentration are separate questions.** A broad opportunity atlas can support a portfolio of multiple niches; simultaneous development of hundreds of products will not produce customers.

## What the totals actually mean

- 1,182 entries are **manually specified product-market hypotheses** (six scoped offers across each of 197 sectors). They often share infrastructure or monetization mechanisms. They are neither 1182 independently proven markets nor 1182 novel revenue models.
- The existing 114 research records (54 repo + 60 earlier fast-cash) remain available. The earlier 18 equivalent pairs are still relevant; the new entries are allowed to be adjacent vertical extensions. Do not add all hypothetical financial outcomes together.
- 21 current market/policy references help check whether vendors, categories and legal routes exist. The original studies retain the 89 and 47 source registers (some URLs may overlap). Exact-offer willingness to pay remains to be discovered.
- All new entries have `market_demand_verified_for_exact_offer=false`. Score and first-sale windows are analyst priors, not odds or evidence of completed transactions. Multiple records use identical business-model defaults; do **not** mistake score decimals for measured precision.

## Two simultaneous opportunity search tracks

**A. Fast cash: 72-hour paid problem-solving tests.** Start with a rotating watchlist of 8–12 buyer segments across at least 5 unrelated sectors. Find explicit requests, problems where the buyer can supply approved sample data, and work whose outcome can be accepted. Pick up to three competing pilots to quote in parallel. The service can be manual initially, but time tracking and fixed scope are mandatory. Keep one paid pilot staffed through completion before committing to extra fulfillment.

**B. Low-touch digital/recurring income:** Test paid download, licensed asset, narrow plugin, or managed monitor ideas with an actual potential customer *before* building the full tool. Target future services where 80–95% of routine delivery can be automated; measure customer acquisition and support separately. Online distribution does not create free sales.

## 72-hour cross-niche sprint

| Time | Action | Decision evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Hours 0–4 | Filter 1,296 records by paid-intent channel, start cost, regulated risk and time to sale. Keep ten potential tests across at least five sectors. | Ten explicit possible buyer problems, not a count of generated ideas. |
| Hours 4–12 | Select three dissimilar offers that can be demonstrated honestly with synthetic or permissioned data. Write fixed scope, price, acceptance test and exclusions. | Three buyer-ready offers with no speculative automation promises. |
| Hours 12–24 | Research visible open requests, permitted communities or channel partners for each. Send relevant responses only where allowed; confirm CASL. | Five qualified buyer contacts/responded requests overall; no mass unsolicited messages. |
| Hours 24–48 | Review replies, price objections and sample files. Offer funded milestones and deliver one minimal useful sample. | Paid order preferred; otherwise actual buyer objections. |
| Hours 48–72 | Secure acceptance if bought. Score routes by gross margin per owner hour, sales friction and repeatability; replace failed offers. | Choose best evidenced line; continue research on the others without overbuilding. |

**Weeks 2–4:** Keep 2–4 unrelated niches in the *validation pipeline* while focusing delivery capacity on paying customers. Each week add 5–10 new qualified problem observations from under-covered sectors. Test only an offer with a concrete buyer path. Release a recurring product only after paid repeat-use evidence. If no sales after 30–40 genuinely matched prospects plus at least two meaningful offer revisions, move to a different buyer problem rather than building speculative infrastructure.

**Months 2–3:** At most three operational business lines until quality, acquisition, net contribution and support are stable. Those can be in different niches; there is no permanent niche preference. Separate sales data, MRR, provider expenses, support logs and human hours per line. Use a dashboard to spot winning sectors. Expand sectors from *actual observed returns*.

## Conditional unit-economics example — not an earnings forecast

See `atlas_unit_economics_examples.csv`. It explicitly separates a zero-sales loss from profit if a specified volume of orders were actually won. It excludes owner wages and income taxes; price, fee allowances, costs, volume and time are illustrations only. The repository's conservative digital product forecasts and the earlier faster B2B model stay intact and separate because they describe different businesses, buyers and workloads.

## Verification and evidence rules

- Broad paid market spending (e.g., Upwork completed-work aggregates) is evidence that services are purchased somewhere, **not** that the precise offer or buyer will pay a new entrant.
- The market source for a sector can be only *adjacent* evidence. For example, a fitness scheduling vendor is not evidence that dentists buy a particular custom integration. Classify accordingly.
- Prices for new offers are illustrative anchors, not scraped customer contract prices. Time-to-sale estimates are *possible* windows, with an unbounded no-sale outcome.
- Do not generate low-value SEO doorway pages, scrape unauthorized sources, send mass commercial messages, create fake social proof, copy proprietary materials, sell franchise IP without a license, or automate professional/safety decisions.
- Prioritize permitted exact-intent client requests, partner referrals, existing verified marketplace demand, and voluntary paid pilots. Reference official fees and eligibility when setting checkout.
- For Canadian businesses, obtain any required registrations, review GST/HST, CASL, privacy, permitted data use, and cross-border tax rules. Merchant-of-record handling of sales taxes does not remove personal income-tax responsibility.
