# Supplier Catalog Operations — commercial reality check
Audit date: 2026-10-31

## Decision
Do not build the 96-item catalog. The original list mixed technical requirements, delivery safeguards, overlapping features and business hypotheses. None has evidence sufficient to label our exact offer a high-probability profit opportunity.

All 96 original IDs and titles were assessed. The result is:
- 4 PAID TEST ONLY — B01, J01, K01 and K05. These are pieces of ONE implementation-service experiment, not four independent markets.
- 37 BUNDLE / INTERNAL — useful controls or deliverables inside a paid service, not independently validated products.
- 35 DEFER UNTIL BUYER — relevant or plausible, but missing exact buyer proof or carrying material expertise/support requirements.
- 20 REJECT AS STANDALONE — do not launch in their generic form for this user's fast, low-input-income objective.

These are judgment-based decisions, not statistical probabilities. A rejected entry might still work for another operator with different expertise, customers or resources. A deferred entry is not automatically a future launch.

## What was verified
39 reviewed source records cover official platform constraints, incumbent capabilities, free alternatives, marketplace reviews, buyer requests and indexed work-history entries. Each audit row identifies its sources, alternatives, remaining human work and the evidence needed before spending.

The provided workbook itself labels all offers as hypotheses and records no exact paying-customer validation. This audit does not conduct interviews, collect payments or measure production performance.

The evidence is asymmetric:
- Technical documentation establishes that a problem or constraint exists, not that a buyer will pay us.
- Competitor prices establish an asking price, not revenue or profit.
- Reviews indicate use and feedback; free plans and review selection prevent treating them as counts of paid customers.
- Buyer requests establish stated demand, not completed payment.
- Indexed completed contracts strengthen adjacent service evidence, but earnings are private and direct page access was restricted for some records.
- No source verifies our acquisition cost, conversion rate, retention, margins or total owner workload.

## Material findings
1. Generic CSV checking and transformation are commoditized. ShopSheets advertises free validators, comparisons, conversions, barcode checks, image checks and other utilities (V11). CatalogWash advertises a free report and a $9 beta cleanup session, with displayed currency not independently established (V12).
2. AI is already inside the competing workflow. Matrixify documents supplier-file conversion and import management through an AI-agent MCP connection (V06). Shelvio advertises mapping, cleaning, saved supplier mappings and plans from US$9.99/month; its listing had zero reviews when checked, so neither adoption nor performance is independently established (V10).
3. Existing software also handles recurring jobs and operational controls (V05, V08, V09). Building scheduling or a dashboard is not itself a differentiator.
4. There is related implementation demand. An agency requested roughly 3,000 products from eight catalogs and a paid sample, with 20–50 proposals. Its body says fixed-price while metadata lists hourly terms, so it is not a clean rate benchmark (V26). An indexed completed bulk-import contract has private earnings (V27).
5. Work is not demonstrated passive. One broader ongoing listing specifies 5–10 hours/week in its body and an advertised US$7–12/hour; metadata conflicts on hours (V28). Another advertises US$25–50/hour but is U.S.-only and includes broader technical catalog/UX work (V39). Neither is a representative market average or an earning promise.
6. The harder work often involves domain facts, identities, inventory semantics and customer decisions (V18, V21, V37, V38). Those requirements cannot be replaced safely by arbitrary AI guesses.

## The only experiment retained
Offer to configure one repeatable supplier-catalog workflow using suitable EXISTING tools. The customer must have a specific unresolved problem, structured data, authority to provide it and a clear definition of a correct result.

K05 is the implementation offer; B01 is its bounded supplier onboarding; K01 is an agency delivery channel; J01 is a possible follow-on ONLY when repeat processing is actually bought.

Before offering custom development, compare the problem with the vendor's own support, native Shopify tools, Matrixify, Stock Sync, Power Query and relevant free tools. If those already solve it adequately, do not manufacture a gap.

This is a service-first experiment, not currently a passive product. If sales, onboarding or exception handling are unacceptable to the owner, the present niche does not meet the stated requirement.

## Evidence gates — recommendations, not probabilities
A. No substantial custom build until at least one real customer funds a defined pilot for a problem not adequately solved by their existing tools.
B. Seek three independent paid pilots and record why the customer chose us; these are management gates, not statistical proof.
C. Require two actual repeat payments for a recurring offer, rather than relying on interest or subscriptions proposed by us.
D. Measure ALL time: acquisition, calls/messages, onboarding, data clarification, processing, QA, support, defects and administration.
E. Calculate contribution after payment/platform costs, hosting, refunds and an explicit value for owner labor. Count work before the invoice, not only run time.
F. Do not call the service low-touch until a representative live pilot meets the owner's time limit. The earlier proposed <=2 service-hours/week at ten established accounts and 95/100 straight-through batches are unachieved targets, not results. Also report total hours including sales.
G. Stop or narrow the offer when free/cheap alternatives win, repeat buyers are absent, or exception labor consumes the margin.

## Change to the previous plan
The 96-item plan remains a research inventory, not a validated build backlog. Suspend its expansion and revenue scenarios as decision evidence. Do not treat the original START/NEXT labels or a long source list as validation.

The next authorized decision should be whether to test ONE paid implementation offer. This document authorizes no outreach, payments, account changes, client-data processing or live-store writes.
