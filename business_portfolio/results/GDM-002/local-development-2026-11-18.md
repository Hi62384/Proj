# GDM-002 local development checkpoint — 2026-11-18

The owner revised the technical gate to permit local development and testing even though a comparative advantage has not been established. Spending and publication still require owner approval; no spending, outreach or publication occurred in this work.

An unpublished Godot 4 project now contains a configurable timetable graph, capacity and FIFO seat ledger, synchronous journey coordinator, Godot Node wrapper and a synthetic 3D example. The example uses 20 passengers, two platforms and a transfer; it can save and replay a trusted local scene and demonstrates recovery and rerouting after a service is disabled. Platform returns are illustrative animation supplied by the example; the coordinator does not simulate physics or verify vehicle positioning.

On Godot 4.5.1, local headless tests passed for graph and ledger contracts (including 1,000 seeded overlap checks), 20-agent journey lifecycle, disruption, lifecycle edge cases, stop closure, Node interface, scene save/replay and scene disruption. The disruption test reported two recovered passengers and all 20 completed. Godot emitted a root-certificate-store warning in the sandbox on these offline runs. An initial demo disruption test failed because its callback counter was captured incorrectly and the test disabled the fast service while a different service was active; the test was corrected to select the intended service and record callbacks, then passed. No tests establish a commercial advantage, real-controller integration, physics, security of untrusted saves, broad engine support or production readiness.

Remaining work includes real movement-controller integration and failure handling, realistic platform feasibility, installation and compatibility QA, performance and fairness evaluation, documentation and rights review. Snapshot version 4 has no migration; cancellation while onboard is not yet supported. Earlier focused engineering time is unknown, and this checkpoint does not infer it from elapsed wall time. No price, revenue or customer demand is established.

A further headless adapter experiment drove a `CharacterBody3D` toward a platform, explicitly carried it with an `AnimatableBody3D` translation, then moved it to an end stop and acknowledged arrival. The first movement test failed three distance checks because the test controller's proportional speed did not approach its target quickly enough; after increasing its bounded gain, it passed. This is one passenger in an empty scene with no collision shapes, not a test of collision-based platform behavior, navigation around obstacles, or a reusable production adapter.

A separate Godot physics scene connected the new coordinator to one `CharacterBody3D` with a capsule collision shape on a translating `AnimatableBody3D` with a box collision shape. The body remained on the moving platform, walked onto a static destination station, and completed its journey after the physical alighting acknowledgment. This passed in one controlled single-passenger scene; it is not evidence for crowds, obstacle avoidance, uneven geometry, automated boarding, failure recovery or production adapter readiness.

Three further controlled physics scenarios passed: four capsule bodies rode the same translating platform and walked to a station; a physical obstacle prevented a passenger's exit until it was removed, with the old alighting request rejected after a new tick; and a passenger on a disabled service walked from the platform to a safe stop before recovery was acknowledged. These scenes start passengers on the platform and use scripted straight-line movement. They do not establish automated boarding, navigation around obstacles, general crowd behavior, or realistic emergency evacuation.

A fresh local Godot project was created with only the add-on copied into its `addons` directory. The copied script configured a graph and opened boarding. The first installation probe printed PASS but also emitted a deferred-node error because the probe freed a node it had scheduled to add; that probe was corrected and rerun without that error. This is a basic path and loading check on Godot 4.5.1, not broad installation or editor compatibility certification. The temporary project was removed after the check.

A single passenger also responded to a boarding request by using `NavigationAgent3D` to walk across a flat navigation mesh onto the collision platform, then rode it and walked off at the station. An initial navigation attempt stayed in place because path-point distance was smaller than the vertical difference between the navigation mesh and the body center; an attempted adjustment incorrectly treated the navigation agent as a spatial node and produced a script error. The corrected flat-scene distance settings passed. This is not a reusable navigation adapter or a test of pathfinding around obstructions or between disconnected navigation regions.

A headless coordinator-only scaling sample used one directed service, capacity 10, immediate simulated acknowledgments, one warmup and five timed complete runs per size on Godot 4.5.1. Total times in microseconds were 20 agents `[2092,1759,5286,1564,1543]`, 100 `[15447,11623,18469,12490,11265]`, and 500 `[118783,177521,138973,175152,162102]`; observed maximum single-tick times in those samples were 3890, 2914 and 14401 microseconds respectively. All synthetic journeys completed. This noisy sample includes event handling but excludes initial request setup, rendering, physics, navigation, integration, other hardware and worst-case graphs. It is not an FPS claim or performance guarantee. The sandbox certificate-store warning was present.

An optional navigation adapter now drives registered CharacterBody3D agents toward game-supplied targets and requires a game-provided physical-arrival predicate before it acknowledges a boarding, alighting or recovery request. It reports timeouts and stale/rejected acknowledgments, and stopping adapter movement does not release a seat. A flat-scene collision/navigation boarding test passed, as did a check that an unverified arrival times out without acknowledgement and an expired request is rejected. The adapter is a starting point with horizontal motion and fixed gravity; realistic navigation, failure policy, object deletion and restoring in-flight movement still require integration work.

The optional adapter also moved four navigation agents along separate lanes onto one collision platform; all four acknowledged boarding after their game-supplied checks, rode together and walked to the destination. This controlled geometry does not demonstrate avoidance in a congested or changing scene. The copied-add-on check was repeated with the adapter included. A safety regression additionally rejected a nonfinite target and verified that a movement cancelled from inside its arrival predicate did not acknowledge the request.

The coordinator now exposes `pending_request(agent)` so a game can reconstruct an outstanding boarding, alighting or recovery request after restoring a snapshot. Its ID and token are preserved; its reported tick is the current coordinator tick. Tests restored pending boarding and recovery requests and restarted an adapter acknowledgment. The game still must restore physical bodies, validate the current world and restart the movement safely; the adapter does not serialize motion.

Onboard cancellation is now a two-stage request: it retains the occupied seat, requests movement to a currently safe endpoint, and marks the journey cancelled only after token-checked recovery acknowledgment. The cancellation intent survives snapshot restore and changing stop availability; stale acknowledgments are rejected. A confirmed boarding cannot be cancelled during that boarding phase and must be retried after departure. The snapshot format is now version 5, with no migration from earlier experimental versions. This behavior passed coordinator tests; a representative physical cancellation scene remains to be tested.

A subsequent single-passenger collision scene also passed the cancellation path: the passenger walked from the translating platform to the destination station, then recovery was acknowledged and the journey became cancelled. This is a controlled scripted exit rather than a general evacuation or crowd test.

A further local scene saved during navigation toward the platform, restored the coordinator, reconstructed its pending request and resumed the adapter using the same physical body; boarding, the collision-platform ride and exit then completed. This confirms the coordinator/adapter handoff in one scene, not serialization of the body, navigation state or vehicle.

The adapter reports a missing body without acknowledging its movement. An explicit `remove_destroyed_agent` coordinator call lets a game assert that a physical NPC is gone and release its queue claim or occupied seat; normal onboard cancellation continues to require safe recovery. Tests covered queued, claimed, boarding-confirmed, onboard and stranded cleanup, duplicate calls, stale callbacks and restored state. This API relies on the game to verify destruction and must not be used to bypass recovery for an existing passenger.

A second controlled collision movement pattern passed: a passenger rode a vertically translating platform and walked onto a raised station before the coordinator accepted alighting.
