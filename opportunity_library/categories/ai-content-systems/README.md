## Commercial thesis

Sell a constrained production or quality workflow rather than a promise to flood platforms with AI output. Reuse schemas and test fixtures, retain source provenance, and price human review explicitly.

**Principal obstacle:** The tool can produce quickly while fact-checking, rights clearance and distribution remain labor-intensive. Platform AI rules are not interchangeable.

These are screened concepts, not verified profitable businesses. Current category/platform sources establish alternatives and constraints, not willingness to pay for our exact products. Test-price ranges, effort and scores are unmeasured planning assumptions.

## Six additional concrete experiments

### CUR-01-01 · Citation-locked knowledge article drafts
**Buyer:** Small support teams  
**Deliverable:** Draft answers only from an approved source library and attach sentence-level source references  
**Mechanism:** Hosted software subscription · **Unvalidated asking-price range:** 39–149 CAD.

**First test:** Sell a reviewed trial of ten articles with a pre-agreed unsupported-claim test
**Distribution path:** Paid design partners or existing accepted workflows; a public SaaS launch alone is not acquisition.
**What can be automated:** Billing events, access, quotas, jobs, telemetry, tested deployments and basic support triage.
**Human work remains:** Security, onboarding, product quality, retention, customer acquisition, incidents and high-risk releases.
**Specific obstacle:** Hallucinated claims and private documents
**Public privacy:** A branded product can avoid face-led promotion. Checkout, invoices, contract documents, registries and mandatory disclosures still need review.
**Advance only when:** Independent paid pilots, repeat usage, cancellation/tenant-isolation tests and an affordable operating reserve.
**Stop or revise:** No scale if retention, support, hosting cost or security cannot sustain the recurring promise.
**Evidence:** Category-level references above; no paid customer for this offer was verified.

### CUR-01-02 · AI content editorial acceptance kit
**Buyer:** Independent publishers  
**Deliverable:** An original checklist, regression examples and scorecard for factuality, rights and repetition  
**Mechanism:** Self-service digital download · **Unvalidated asking-price range:** 29–79 CAD.

**First test:** Ask three editors to audit the same sample before offering the complete editable kit
**Distribution path:** Permitted original-product marketplace, useful sample, opt-in audience or a specialist distribution partner.
**What can be automated:** Reproducible builds, compatibility tests, versioned downloads, licence records and documented onboarding.
**Human work remains:** Original product QA, licensing, customer discovery, accessibility, refunds and compatibility updates.
**Specific obstacle:** A checklist is not a quality guarantee
**Public privacy:** A branded product can avoid face-led promotion. Checkout, invoices, contract documents, registries and mandatory disclosures still need review.
**Advance only when:** Three independent paid users who can complete the advertised task; test support time before adding variants.
**Stop or revise:** Revise after a bounded qualified-traffic test with no purchases; do not respond by mass-producing more files.
**Evidence:** Category-level references above; no paid customer for this offer was verified.

### CUR-01-04 · Webinar-to-resource pilot
**Buyer:** B2B training companies  
**Deliverable:** Transform one authorized webinar into a reviewed guide, chapter index and social excerpts  
**Mechanism:** Fixed-scope service · **Unvalidated asking-price range:** 250–600 CAD.

**First test:** Ask one buyer to pay for a capped transformation with factual and quotation approval
**Distribution path:** Relevant explicit purchase requests, permissioned introductions, or a small partner pilot.
**What can be automated:** Intake validation, deterministic transformations, test fixtures, packaging and draft delivery notes.
**Human work remains:** Qualifying buyers, scope agreement, ambiguous data, output acceptance, confidentiality and disputes.
**Specific obstacle:** Editing time and speaker permissions
**Public privacy:** A brand can reduce personal marketing. Contracts and marketplaces such as Upwork can disclose a verified name to customers.
**Advance only when:** Two independent accepted paid jobs, bounded scope and measured contribution after sales/delivery time before expanding.
**Stop or revise:** Pause expansion when custom exceptions exceed the quote or focused buyer conversations reveal no willingness to pay.
**Evidence:** Category-level references above; no paid customer for this offer was verified.

## Validation questions still unanswered

What is the real acquisition cost and all-in support time? Which seller and identity details will buyers see? Which rights, data and professional rules constrain delivery?
