## Research the opportunity before expanding the specification

1. Define the target buyer or audience, exact use or purchase situation, present alternative, and reason to pay. Search real workflows, existing paid categories, independent requests/reviews and contrary evidence before generating ideas. For original books, games or artwork, investigate audience, genre and distinctive creative quality rather than inventing software-like exclusivity requirements.
2. Inspect the strongest complete free/native/open-source workflow, practical combinations of tools, reasonable AI-assisted DIY, relevant paid offers and the cheapest capable comparable paid alternative. Compare the whole buyer outcome, quality, setup effort, compatibility, rights, reliability, support burden and total cost. Free primitives do not automatically equal a finished product; a complete equally convenient free near-clone without material added value is a serious failure.
3. Establish a specific buyer-relevant advantage. Where a capable comparable paid offer exists, propose a sustainable price below the cheapest verified comparable alternative after platform fees, refunds, support and delivery costs. If this is not economically plausible, narrow or reject rather than fabricate a lower sustainable price. Distinguish creative differentiation and noncomparable business models from forced feature comparisons.
4. Check technical feasibility, content/data/IP rights, Canadian seller/access constraints where relevant, likely distribution and acquisition access, initial cash, owner effort, support/maintenance and the total 2–21-day scope. Identify duplicates or reusable existing work from the supplied context. Do not assume unseen repository projects were inspected.
5. Run a **fresh disconfirming research pass** before the recommendation: new search terms, recent native capabilities, complete free variants, lower-cost substitutes, unsolved acquisition difficulty, unrealistic maintenance or missing rights. Record the strongest objections and the evidence that resolves or sustains them. A second model's agreement is not market evidence.
6. Record a concise PASS / FAIL / UNKNOWN ledger for buyer value, demand context, complete alternatives, price economics, technical/rights feasibility, channel access, owner workload and cash/time fit. Separate presently knowable decisive unknowns from normal future market uncertainty.

### TEST-FIRST GO and honest verdicts

A **TEST-FIRST GO** means credible category/workflow demand or a paying creative audience, meaningful buyer value, feasible rights/economics and bounded downside make an experiment worthwhile. It does not claim that this unreleased offer already has buyers. Keep it **TEST-FIRST / commercially unvalidated** and preserve `commercial_validation`.

Use `research_verdict` and a body explanation: `GO` for a worthwhile bounded project; `CONDITIONAL_PROOF_ONLY` for one decisive technical or outcome advantage needing a small early comparison; `RESEARCH_BLOCKED` for an inaccessible presently knowable decisive fact; `NO_GO` for an evidenced failure that has no credible in-scope remedy. Older records may contain `GO_TO_PHASE_B`; preserve that as historical wording, not a new phase number. A **Future TESTABLE hypothesis** such as first sales, conversion or an unknown creator's popularity is not itself a prelaunch failure. Specify later measurement and stop/pivot criteria instead. Never force a positive verdict, require fabricated paid buyers or manufacture a replacement merely to show activity.

## Detailed Phase 1 specification

When creating or substantively revising a viable candidate, write an implementation-ready specification with enough detail for Codex to build it without guessing the core product. Include:

- Product purpose, target audience, concrete scenarios, buyer value, descriptive title and a small complete first release.
- Features and behavior: inputs/outputs, user flows, screens or commands, configuration, integration boundaries, persistence, accessibility where relevant, error cases and recovery. For content/creative products use the equivalent structure, style, production rules, audience promise and quality criteria.
- Architecture or production approach, data/assets/content needed, licensed dependencies, supported versions and known compatibility assumptions. Phase 2 will expand the accepted port/format matrix and must update this specification accordingly.
- Component-level work breakdown with dependencies, relative effort, acceptance criteria and test inputs; clear separation between a first falsification proof, production, QA, documentation and packaging.
- Quality and verification: representative positive/negative cases, usability, performance constraints where meaningful, independent first-use checks and observable completion criteria. Do not substitute invented tests or generic “make it polished” for measurable results.
- Exact deliverables, source/build/package locations under the existing sibling `work/`, customer documentation/demo requirements, versioning, licensing and support scope.
- Explicit exclusions, conditional assumptions, biggest risks, mitigation, stop/pivot criteria, remaining research questions and a total schedule across phases 1–6 fitting 2–21 days.
- Phase 4 build checklist and provisional Phase 5/6 execution checklists. Leave unresearched release and marketing specifics clearly pending their own Phase 2/3 research.

If the candidate fails, keep the same selected project record, explain the evidence and the smallest appropriate next decision. A research record can document NO_GO or a blocker without pretending an active build was approved. Do not erase completed existing work or switch the project's identity.
