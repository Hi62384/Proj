# Temporary independent transit integration

Original synthetic test project with a game-owned character controller and two physical platforms. The test fixture is adapted from the product integration fixture and uses a local copy of the current candidate add-on. It is not an existing third-party game or evidence of general controller compatibility. Run Godot with `--headless --path <this folder> --script res://test_transfer.gd`.

`test_midride_restore.gd` saves the coordinator, passenger and first platform during travel, reconstructs those objects, resumes the physical ride and completes the transfer. The second platform and rest of the scene remain in memory. Both fixtures passed on standard Godot 4.4.1, 4.5.1 and 4.7.2 on Windows x86-64; root-certificate-store warnings were emitted. The fixtures are adapted from existing product tests and do not prove integration with an independently developed game, arbitrary world persistence, or general crowd navigation. The copied add-on files match the current review candidate by hash, excluding generated UID files.

`test_interruption.gd` disables a service while the passenger rides, checks the occupied seat remains held, moves the platform back to a known safe dock and waits for the game-owned controller to physically exit before acknowledging recovery and releasing the seat. It passed on the same three versions. The route and safe stop are scripted; this is not general vehicle replanning or emergency evacuation.

`test_onboard_cancellation.gd` uses the same physical safe-dock arrangement to cancel the passenger's journey while aboard. It verifies that the seat remains occupied until physical recovery and that the final journey state is cancelled. It passed on all three versions; this is one scripted cancellation path.

Run `python run_checks.py <godot-console-executable>` to execute the eight headless cases with isolated user data. The runner passed 8/8 on 4.7.2. A separate `test_visual_transfer.gd` renders the same transfer with primitive meshes; hidden 4.7.2 OpenGL captures at 1152×648 were inspected for the passenger boarding, riding and reaching the destination. The first camera framing was too distant and was adjusted before the final capture. These are automated captures of a synthetic scene, not hands-on interaction, editor-gizmo acceptance or an existing game's visual integration.

`test_full_world_restore.gd` saves the coordinator, passenger and both platform positions mid-ride, destroys the test world, recreates both platforms and the fixed dock surfaces, restores the passenger and coordinator, and completes the trip. It passed individually on standard Godot 4.4.1, 4.5.1 and 4.7.2; the updated five-case runner passed 5/5 on 4.7.2. Fixed dock geometry is recreated from the fixture rather than serialized, and the route and timing remain scripted. This is not general game-world persistence.

`test_navigation_adapter.gd` adds the optional navigation adapter alongside the game-owned controller cases. Adapted from the product's navigation fixture, it exercises navigation to a physical platform, verified boarding, a physical ride and adapter-controlled movement to the destination with verified alighting. The navigation mesh covers a fixed straight route and the platform movement is scripted. The fixture passed on standard Godot 4.4.1, 4.5.1 and 4.7.2; the expanded runner passed all six cases on 4.7.2. This is a bounded synthetic scene, not compatibility proof for arbitrary game controllers or dynamic navigation geometry.

`test_navigation_recovery.gd` adapts the product's recovery fixture to the same temporary project: after a scripted physical ride, service disruption triggers a recovery request and the navigation adapter verifies arrival at the safe destination before acknowledging it. It passed on standard Godot 4.4.1, 4.5.1 and 4.7.2, and the updated seven-case runner passed 7/7 on 4.7.2. The scene uses a fixed navigation mesh, one passenger and a scripted platform route; it does not establish general obstacle avoidance or safe-stop selection.


The complete seven-case runner subsequently passed 7/7 on each of Godot 4.4.1 and 4.5.1 as well as 4.7.2. The temporary add-on predates the later editor-only marker appearance fix; its game runtime is unchanged by that fix.

`measure_staggered_twenty_physics.gd` instruments the product's scripted twenty-passenger physical transfer to sample Godot's physics-process monitor. It is not part of the eight-case runner and is not an FPS benchmark; see `../PERFORMANCE_PHYSICAL_2026-11-26.md` for the run, limitations and reproduction command.


`test_blocked_crowd_restore.gd` adapts the product's four-passenger blocked-recovery fixture. Three passengers physically exit while one remains stranded with a seat held; it snapshots that mixed state, replaces the coordinator and adapter, verifies the completed and stranded states and the occupied seat, then removes the obstacle and verifies the last passenger physically reaches safety before acknowledgment. The individual case passed on standard Godot 4.4.1, 4.5.1 and 4.7.2; the eight-case runner passed 8/8 on 4.7.2. The fixed obstacle, scripted platform and reused passenger bodies do not prove arbitrary crowd avoidance, full world restoration or safe-stop selection.
