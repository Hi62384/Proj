Find the best worthwhile, bounded product opportunity for the appended selected niche. Use the appended Empty project or current Phase 1 record as the destination. ## Mandatory finalist verification — research depth before project creation

**Do not finalize a GO or produce the implementation-ready project plan until the BEST candidate survives an evidence-backed recheck comparable to Phase 1's separate verification prompt.** The 2–21-day total project scope makes early discovery of fatal commercial or build problems more valuable than generating a long speculative specification. A long duration, a large source list, or confidence in the initial idea is not itself research quality.

**Research in stages, not indiscriminately.** First identify buyers and several genuinely different opportunities or significant variations, rapidly eliminate obvious failures, and rank serious finalists. Then perform the following **deep verification on the strongest finalist**, including any replacement finalist after a failure. Avoid spending equivalent effort on every quickly discarded idea, but do not skip the decisive checks for the winner.

1. **Independent buyer evidence:** Directly inspect multiple relevant dated buyer/user discussions, requests, issue threads, detailed reviews or comparable purchasing signals where available. Record what the buyer actually needs, alternatives used, why a buyer might pay for this particular outcome, and counterevidence such as satisfaction with free solutions. Separate observed buyer behavior from inferred interest and hypothetical conversions. Creative works use accurately comparable genre/audience signals without assuming a new original work has presales.
2. **End-to-end competitive evidence:** Inspect the strongest COMPLETE free/native/open-source/freemium solution or combined workflow, realistic AI-assisted DIY, the closest paid competitors and the cheapest capable comparable paid alternative. Compare real buyer jobs, integration/setup time, exact licensing/terms, supported versions, completeness, prices and ongoing customer effort. Open primary descriptions, documentation and prices instead of relying on search snippets. Explain a concrete material advantage or distinctive original creative appeal.
3. **Price, feasibility and access:** Recalculate sustainable below-comparable-paid pricing where applicable, relevant fees/refunds/support, plausible net contribution, a realistic zero-sales downside, customer-discovery route and owner hours. Verify critical technical prerequisites, content/IP rights, Canadian seller/distribution eligibility and the implementation scope. Unknown future first sales are TESTABLE after release, not a Phase 1 failure.
4. **Separate adversarial search:** AFTER the promising initial finding, perform a NEW skeptical search using different job-to-be-done phrases and competitor terms. Attempt to disprove demand, find full free substitutes or cheaper capable products, uncover impractical acquisition/maintenance, licensing barriers or impossible promises. Record **at least three serious, material objections or falsification attempts**, with what direct evidence supports, refutes or leaves each unresolved. Do not invent objections merely to hit a number.
5. **Build-readiness audit:** Verify that the detailed product scope can be implemented by Codex without guessing key features: essential workflows/outputs, dependencies, negative/edge cases, required assets/licenses, acceptance tests, proof-first task, and realistic time for research + build + release + initial marketing together.

**Depth target for a substantive finalist:** Aim to directly open and inspect approximately **8–15 relevant, distinct source pages in total** across buyer-side evidence, free/AI and paid alternatives, official legal/platform facts and skeptical counterevidence. It is a **coverage guide, not a mandatory count or time quota**. Fewer sources may be justified for narrow markets, and browsing 15 irrelevant pages is not success. Reuse high-quality prior material when current and relevant, but independently verify decision-changing facts. If key sources cannot be accessed, report the precise UNKNOWN; do not invent checks or promote unverified facts to PASS.

**Required evidence packet in the project body BEFORE final GO:** Include a table with each decision-critical claim, exact direct source URL, observation/access date, finding, and whether it was initial evidence, independently RECHECKED evidence or UNKNOWN. Include a PASS / FAIL / UNKNOWN ledger for buyer value, demand context, complete free/AI substitutes, cheapest capable paid competitor and net economics, feasible acquisition, rights/technology, owner effort and total time/cash downside. Document the **three strongest counterfindings** and explain whether the finalist remains worthwhile after each. Include a brief readiness assessment of implementation acceptance criteria.

If a finalist fails a real material gate, **evaluate the next-best finalist or a substantially different viable variation using this same deep-check standard** before settling on NO_GO. Do not write a canonical project just to satisfy the output. If a finalist passes as a modest TEST-FIRST opportunity, write the full project specification honestly and label future exact sales/conversions as unvalidated—not grounds for automatic failure.

**Stopping rule:** Finish when the material evidence is sufficient to justify or reject the bounded candidate, not after 5, 30 or 60 minutes on a clock. If the task lacks credible evidence, say what remains unresolved instead of claiming an approved opportunity. The actual research trail matters much more than wall-clock elapsed time.

## Candidate rejection and niche-wide NO_GO exit gate

**Critical distinction:** A finalist failing its deep verification is an **IDEA_NO_GO**, not proof that the selected niche contains no worthwhile projects. Do not end the Phase 1 run merely because the first or most interesting candidate failed. Continue product discovery **within the same invocation**, using the reasons for failure to make the next search materially different and more informed. Do not ask the owner to send an additional prompt to recover from an ordinary failed candidate.

### Discovery recovery sequence

1. **Build a meaningful shortlist BEFORE fixating on a favorite.** Start with actual buyers, paid categories, recurrent tasks and creative audiences; identify several distinct jobs-to-be-done, product types or buyer segments within the selected niche. Keep a brief candidate ledger with audience, paid/free alternatives, potential value, first major concern and preliminary ranking. For a broad niche, consider roughly 12–25 quick opportunities, shortlist around 3–6, and deeply vet the strongest 2–3 as warranted. These are **coverage guides, not quotas**; narrow niches or unusually strong primary evidence can justify fewer.
2. **When finalist A fails, explain WHY before pivoting.** Record the material disqualifier and direct support. Determine whether a significant buyer/feature/pricing/format variation actually resolves the failure; do not salvage an equivalent free clone by giving it a new name. If a credible variation remains, challenge that variation independently rather than assuming the new label fixes everything.
3. **If no meaningful variation survives, investigate finalist B** with a *different buyer job, category, distribution angle or defensible value proposition*. Reuse relevant facts but perform fresh searches and the same substantive buyer/competition/economics/rights scrutiny. If B fails, inspect a genuinely distinct C or a newly discovered demand-led opportunity where the niche has sufficient breadth. Do not simply list rejected ideas without checking the actual potentially viable alternatives.
4. **If many ideas fail for the same reason**, change the search strategy. Inspect who is actually paying for specialized outcomes, unmet friction in capable tools, narrower professional segments, adjacent formats and customer-acquisition routes. Do not keep generating cosmetic versions of the same commodity product. Classify each failed pathway as evidenced fatal weakness, potentially repairable, not yet verified or low-priority—not as proof that all possible ideas are impossible.
5. **Approve the strongest TEST-FIRST opportunity only if its independent deep checks survive.** Exact sales by the not-yet-launched product are a future test; a real fatal free-equivalence, cost, technical, rights or buyer-access problem remains disqualifying. Do not relax standards after several rejections to guarantee a GO.

### NO_GO must be justified at the *niche* level, not only for one product

Only conclude **"NO_GO — no suitable opportunity identified in this research run"** after a meaningful search across the important **distinct buyer/product pathways** that were realistically available within the scope. As a practical guide in a broad niche, provide a clear comparison of **at least three substantively different finalist directions** that received actual evidence checks, or explain with specific sources why fewer existed or could be assessed. This is an evidence sufficiency check, **not a numeric pass/fail quota** and not a claim that every imaginable business was exhausted.

A final non-approval report must include:
- The shortlisted directions and any meaningful variations; actual buyer problem and independent evidence investigated for each.
- For each deeply examined finalist, the strongest complete free/AI/paid alternatives, financial/customer access considerations, **specific sourced disqualifier or decisive unknown**, and how the verdict differed from the earlier candidate.
- What new direction was explored **after** the first rejection; do not stop at "the initial product failed."
- Why no other seriously promising candidate is reasonably identifiable from the research, which parts of the niche remain unexamined, and the strongest next lead for a later attempt.
- A distinction between **NO_GO** (evidenced fatal fail), **RESEARCH_BLOCKED** (important presently knowable fact inaccessible), and **CONDITIONAL_PROOF_ONLY** (one small decisive technical side-by-side proof). Do not use niche-wide NO_GO merely because a product has no prelaunch conversion data or because a source was inaccessible.

**If research/tool/context limits prevent properly examining the next viable finalist, say that the search is incomplete** and list the precise next candidate and required research; do not falsely report that the niche has no opportunity. Never fabricate sources, competitor findings, buyer evidence or completed searches. A truthful no-go is allowed when this exit gate is genuinely satisfied. There is no promise of eventual success and no endless background loop.

## Research the opportunity before expanding the specification

1. Define the target buyer or audience, exact use or purchase situation, present alternative, and reason to pay. Search real workflows, existing paid categories, independent requests/reviews and contrary evidence before generating ideas. For original books, games or artwork, investigate audience, genre and distinctive creative quality rather than inventing software-like exclusivity requirements.
2. Inspect the strongest complete free/native/open-source workflow, practical combinations of tools, reasonable AI-assisted DIY, relevant paid offers and the cheapest capable comparable paid alternative. Compare the whole buyer outcome, quality, setup effort, compatibility, rights, reliability, support burden and total cost. Free primitives do not automatically equal a finished product; a complete equally convenient free near-clone without material added value is a serious failure.
3. Establish a specific buyer-relevant advantage. Where a capable comparable paid offer exists, propose a sustainable price below the cheapest verified comparable alternative after platform fees, refunds, support and delivery costs. If this is not economically plausible, narrow or reject rather than fabricate a lower sustainable price. Distinguish creative differentiation and noncomparable business models from forced feature comparisons.
4. Check technical feasibility, content/data/IP rights, Canadian seller/access constraints where relevant, likely distribution and acquisition access, initial cash, owner effort, support/maintenance and the total 2–21-day scope. Identify duplicates or reusable existing work from the supplied context. Do not assume unseen repository projects were inspected.
5. Run a **fresh disconfirming research pass** before the recommendation: new search terms, recent native capabilities, complete free variants, lower-cost substitutes, unsolved acquisition difficulty, unrealistic maintenance or missing rights. Record the strongest objections and the evidence that resolves or sustains them. A second model's agreement is not market evidence.
6. Record a concise PASS / FAIL / UNKNOWN ledger for buyer value, demand context, complete alternatives, price economics, technical/rights feasibility, channel access, owner workload and cash/time fit. Separate presently knowable decisive unknowns from normal future market uncertainty.

### TEST-FIRST GO and honest verdicts

A **TEST-FIRST GO** means credible category/workflow demand or a paying creative audience, meaningful buyer value, feasible rights/economics and bounded downside make an experiment worthwhile. It does not claim that this unreleased offer already has buyers. Keep it **TEST-FIRST / commercially unvalidated** and preserve `commercial_validation`.

Use `research_verdict` and a body explanation: `GO` for a worthwhile bounded project; `CONDITIONAL_PROOF_ONLY` for one decisive technical or outcome advantage needing a small early comparison; `RESEARCH_BLOCKED` for an inaccessible presently knowable decisive fact; `NO_GO` for an evidenced failure that has no credible in-scope remedy. Older records may contain `GO_TO_PHASE_B`; preserve that as historical wording, not a new phase number. A **Future TESTABLE hypothesis** such as first sales, conversion or an unknown creator's popularity is not itself a prelaunch failure. Specify later measurement and stop/pivot criteria instead. Never force a positive verdict, require fabricated paid buyers or manufacture a replacement merely to show activity.

## Detailed Phase 1 specification

When creating or substantively revising a viable candidate, write an implementation-ready specification with enough detail for Codex to build it without guessing the core product. Include:

- Product purpose, target audience, concrete scenarios, buyer value, descriptive title and a small complete first release.
- Features and behavior: inputs/outputs, user flows, screens or commands, configuration, integration boundaries, persistence, accessibility where relevant, error cases and recovery. For content/creative products use the equivalent structure, style, production rules, audience promise and quality criteria.
- Architecture or production approach, data/assets/content needed, licensed dependencies, supported versions and known compatibility assumptions. Phase 2 will expand the accepted port/format matrix and must update this specification accordingly.
- Component-level work breakdown with dependencies, relative effort, acceptance criteria and test inputs; clear separation between a first falsification proof, production, QA, documentation and packaging.
- Quality and verification: representative positive/negative cases, usability, performance constraints where meaningful, independent first-use checks and observable completion criteria. Do not substitute invented tests or generic “make it polished” for measurable results.
- Exact deliverables, source/build/package locations under the existing sibling `work/`, customer documentation/demo requirements, versioning, licensing and support scope.
- Explicit exclusions, conditional assumptions, biggest risks, mitigation, stop/pivot criteria, remaining research questions and a total schedule across phases 1–6 fitting 2–21 days.
- Phase 4 build checklist and provisional Phase 5/6 execution checklists. Leave unresearched release and marketing specifics clearly pending their own Phase 2/3 research.

If the candidate fails, keep the same selected project record, explain the evidence and the smallest appropriate next decision. A research record can document NO_GO or a blocker without pretending an active build was approved. Do not erase completed existing work or switch the project's identity.
