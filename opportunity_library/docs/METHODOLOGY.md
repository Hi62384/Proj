# Research and ranking methodology

## What this update is

A reorganized, reproducible research library: **36 primary categories, 144 subcategories, 197 inherited industry slices, 1,296 preserved source records and 216 additional offer hypotheses**. Two of the additions are clearly isolated personal-capital research activities, not businesses. The remaining 1,510 records are candidate business offers, not validated independent markets.

The 216 additions are individually specified buyers, outputs, barriers, test-price ranges and first experiments. They are informed by **69 freshly consulted primary references** covering paid software categories, marketplaces, platform rules, data access, identity and Canadian obligations. A paid competitor proves an asking price/category exists; it does not prove that our niche, positioning or new seller will attract customers. No customer interviews, paid tests, private marketplace analytics or launch results were obtained for this update.

## How records are organized

Each record has one primary category and one subcategory, with industry and secondary mechanism tags. This avoids copying one idea into many folders and accidentally counting it as many businesses. All original IDs and their data sources remain intact. The 197 sector slices are not 197 separate monetization mechanisms. For example, a margin workbook for a cafe and one for a contractor can share a calculation engine but need separate buyer evidence.

`data/classifications.json` freezes the assignments for the older records. The 114 legacy records were assigned individually using their actual IDs/titles. Expanded records prioritize explicit product/task terms and otherwise use their stated industry. The original industry home is retained as a secondary tag when a task-specific category is more precise. A `domain-default-review` assignment is explicitly flagged where a precise subcategory could not be inferred. These are organizational judgements, not newly verified demand. Review and edit the assignments rather than assuming a classifier has established business viability.

Some legacy rows are indexes pointing to the full original research; their missing buyer details or workload estimates remain missing. Existing forecasts are retained as source assumptions, not silently improved. The old overlap crosswalk includes references from earlier indexing and is **not used to merge, count unique markets, or assign validation**. New related-workflow links are explicit and do not assert exact duplicates. Similar ideas remain traceable without being presented as independent proof of demand.

## Two ranking lenses, not one magic score

The **Low-touch & public privacy** lens weights passivity 25%, profit potential 25%, public-privacy fit 20%, acquisition feasibility 15%, speed 10%, and evidence 5%. The **Fast cash** lens uses speed 35%, profit potential 25%, routine fulfillment automation 20%, evidence 10%, and scale 10%.

Every input is a 1–5 editorial planning prior. Weighted totals are rounded integers on a 0–100 scale. Ratings primarily reflect business mechanism and known operating burdens, not measured conversion or success probabilities. Financial/security/health and inherited review flags reduce passivity/privacy ratings rather than receiving a speculative earnings bonus. Audience/editorial models retain manual-work penalties. Personal interests, previous projects and favorite software add **zero** ranking points.

A 4 in public privacy means a potentially brand-led product with little need for personal marketing; it does **not** mean that 80% of identity is hidden. A high profit-potential score means the mechanism can support valuable repeat usage at scale, not that profitable customers are easy to acquire. Small score differences are ties; actual paid evidence should dominate priors. All exact-offer paid-validation fields start false. High scores never unlock deployment or spending.

## Automation and passive income are different

**Fulfillment automation** covers a defined repetitive delivery workflow. **Passivity** concerns the entire operation after stabilization: acquisition, onboarding, support, source changes, editorial verification, security, refunds, billing reconciliation and incidents. A download can be automatically delivered while marketing consumes many hours each week. Recurring billing without recurring customer value is not a sustainable subscription.

New setup/weekly hour ranges are class-level hypotheses, not measured results. Inherited hour/price/profit estimates stay explicitly labeled as older assumptions. Every first-sale range permits a no-sale outcome. New expected monthly profit is deliberately unknown. Do not add the conditional profit fields across hundreds of catalogue entries.

## Unit economics: calculate only after defining the offer

For a business, contribution per sale = price excluding sales tax − payment/platform fees − refunds/chargeback allowance − variable production/API/delivery cost − directly attributable acquisition cost. Monthly operating profit subtracts fixed expenses. Owner-time-adjusted profit additionally subtracts **all** owner hours at a deliberately chosen hourly value. Include unsuccessful experiments, support, fulfilment and sales time.

Reinvestment is already an expense when incurred; do not subtract it twice. A payout settles transactions and is not another sale. Tax collected is not revenue. A tax cash reserve is not a tax calculation. Foreign currency prices and fees need actual conversion when incurred. Starting with existing equipment/free software does not mean legal registration or seller approval is free.

For investment research, fees, drawdowns, liquidity and capital loss belong to a **separate capital-risk framework**, not customer sales or recurring business profit. The library creates no trading connection, personal recommendation or expected-return estimate. [OSC](SOURCES.md#r-osc), [market-data access](SOURCES.md#r-alphavantage).

## Evidence ladder and decision rules

**Concept → source-backed problem → permissioned buyer conversation → paid pilot → accepted delivery → repeat paid use → sustainable operations.** A vendor price, job listing, positive comment, free download or algorithmic score is not a completed step further up this ladder.

For each next test, record a source URL/date, the actual buyer problem, their alternatives, permitted acquisition route, prototype scope, accepted price/currency, worst-case spending, delivery/privacy limits and a stop date. Require customer authority before handling private material. Treat government and platform rules as constraints to verify for the exact seller/jurisdiction/product, not just generic category checkboxes.

After three independent paid uses or comparable paid repeat behavior, replace priors with actual contribution, acquisition cost, support time and churn. This is a small early signal, not statistical proof. Stop or revise when no relevant buyer pays, custom scope absorbs margin, source rights are unclear, or customer promises exceed capacity. The goal is net contribution per human hour, not maximum storefront count.

## Research freshness and reproducibility

The new source register records inspection date, retrieval method, claim and limitation. Some pages were read through official search excerpts; this is noted and is weaker than a complete legal interpretation. Original source registers are preserved as inherited research, **not claimed freshly rechecked**. Revisit pricing, identity, AI-content, automation and distribution terms before each launch and whenever a provider changes them.

`python -m opportunity_library.build` regenerates the catalogue, CSVs, category notes and offline browser from source JSON without network calls. `--check` reports drift without writing. Rebuilding or the existing weekly draft action does **not** refresh live market research. New facts require human-reviewed source entries. Draft and source generation never authorizes publication, messages, spending or financial transactions.
