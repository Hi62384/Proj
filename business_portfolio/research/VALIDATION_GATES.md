# Go / narrow / defer gates
These are decision rules, not estimated probabilities or guarantees.

**G0 — real scope:** named buyer/problem, existing alternative checked, no geography/credential mismatch, data rights and a valid acquisition route. Missing proof means research only.

**G1 — skill and cost:** an operator can demonstrate the exact failure case using fictional data; required production access/plan limits/costs are documented. Owner incremental cash defaults to zero and every commitment is inside CAD100. A free prototype does not pass a hosted-software cost gate.

**G2 — small acquisition experiment:** project-specific outcome/proof/CTA and eight or fewer eligible attempts, with at most four unpaid hours for a service; existing products use two placements/three hours; deferred-product discovery uses two hours. User approval is required before sending, publishing or spending. No serious signal at the cap means pause/revise, not more speculative development.

**G3 — paid pilot:** genuine independent customer, funded bounded scope or approved payment arrangement, acceptance conditions and lawful scoped access. Do not call free checkout, a waitlist, a proposal or a verbal promise payment. A commitment and a collected receipt are separate evidence fields.

**G4 — profitable repetition:** at least three independent paid pilots as a management checkpoint, positive measured contribution after all time/costs and no unacceptable failure/support burden. These numbers are not statistical proof. For a recurring offer, require actual repeat payments and observed maintenance demand; do not presume renewal.

**G5 — advertising/productization:** only consider wider promotion, packs or software when the repeated problem and economics warrant it. A tiny ad experiment needs approved total-budget controls and measured funnel assumptions. A software product needs paid design partners, a feasible funded cost/security plan and a narrower scope than a generic multi-tenant platform. Never compromise quality/security just to claim a CAD100 build.

## Red flags that override an attractive price
Unclear consent/data rights; required production licence outside the plan; payment or irreversible financial actions; unearned expertise/partner claims; guaranteed revenue/passivity promises; unknown hosted operations; broad multi-app rescue at a tiny fixed budget; a buyer demanding unlimited support; false attribution; expired/ineligible job listings; or refusing to define acceptance. Decline or isolate a paid diagnostic rather than silently accept these risks.

## Progress integrity
Work state, commercial evidence and spending permission are independent. “Published” is not “paid”, “funded” is not “settled”, “tested with a fixture” is not “production proven”, and “owner chose a project” is not “owner authorized every purchase”. Keep existing completed work and read the newest private checkpoint before repeating uncertain external effects.
