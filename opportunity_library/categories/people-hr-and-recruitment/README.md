## Commercial thesis

Administrative automation and candidate-controlled resources can be bounded. Test an actual recruiter or HR workflow while retaining human hiring decisions.

**Principal obstacle:** Protected traits, fairness, applicant data, surveillance and employment law make autonomous selection and intrusive monitoring unsuitable.

These are screened concepts, not verified profitable businesses. Current category/platform sources establish alternatives and constraints, not willingness to pay for our exact products. Test-price ranges, effort and scores are unmeasured planning assumptions.

## Six additional concrete experiments

### CUR-34-01 · Recruitment status export reconciler
**Buyer:** Small recruiters  
**Deliverable:** Match authorized candidate-stage exports with no automated ranking or hiring decisions  
**Mechanism:** Connector or integration · **Unvalidated asking-price range:** 79–249 CAD.

**First test:** Use fictional candidates and a privacy-reviewed read-only pilot
**Distribution path:** Explicit authorized integration problem, native permitted marketplace or established implementation partner.
**What can be automated:** Schema contracts, import/export, retry/deduplication, diagnostics and consented entitlement checks.
**Human work remains:** Credentials/permissions, initial mapping, provider changes, support and production approval.
**Specific obstacle:** Applicant privacy and discriminatory effects
**Public privacy:** A branded product can avoid face-led promotion. Checkout, invoices, contract documents, registries and mandatory disclosures still need review.
**Advance only when:** Accepted read-only pilot, least privilege, rollback tests and paid repeat-use proof.
**Stop or revise:** Pause when API terms, maintenance, rejected permissions or custom configurations defeat reliability.
**Evidence:** Category-level references above; no paid customer for this offer was verified.
