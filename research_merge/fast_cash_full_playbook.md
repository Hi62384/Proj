# Fast online income: research and launch playbook

Research checked: 2026-10-12. All business pricing/forecasts CAD unless a source explicitly quotes another currency.

## Decision
Start O01 (a tightly scoped reporting-automation service). Add genuinely useful recurring care and then O05 (a licensed reusable toolkit). Add O03 (safe agency monitoring) only after paid demand. O02/O04 are alternative offers, not five businesses to launch at once. No sales, accounts, customer interviews or deployment have been verified.

## Scoring and evidence
Score = 35% speed + 25% profit + 20% automation + 10% evidence/acquisition route + 10% scale, each rated 1–5 and normalized to 100. Rankings are judgement calls; nearby scores are not meaningful precision. E1 is aggregate/contracted spending, E2 paid vendor/category evidence, E3 requests/listings, E4 enabling policy/infrastructure. None proves this user's exact offer will sell. M3 figures are conditional active-business illustrations; all ideas can earn zero.

## Budget envelopes

### CAD 0
Setup cap 0; tools/delivery 0; acquisition 0; professional-review reserve 0; untouched reserve 0. Use existing equipment and a local demo. Permitted introductions/requested-work replies; client funds paid tools. No paid ads, paid job credits, new hosting or speculative software. Registration/eligibility costs may make a literal $0 launch impossible.

### CAD 100
Setup cap 0; tools/delivery 25; acquisition 40; professional-review reserve 0; untouched reserve 35. Keep the demo local; use a small capped acquisition test, reserve for paid-client delivery. Proposal credits cost money; do not assume 40 paid-platform applications fit this budget.

### CAD 500
Setup cap 50; tools/delivery 75; acquisition 100; professional-review reserve 150; untouched reserve 125. Best test envelope: one primary service, limited acquisition and targeted review; keep most spending gated. Professional-review allocation is a reserve, not a quote. Spend only for actual local requirements and paid work.

### CAD 2,000
Setup cap 75; tools/delivery 150; acquisition 200; professional-review reserve 400; untouched reserve 1175. Use the same narrow offer. Preserve runway; spend on needed professional review and measurable delivery/partner tests. Do not spend $2,000 building five products or buying untested ads. Release reserve only after payment and unit-margin evidence.

## Five complete launch blueprints

### O01 — Recurring-report automation sprint

**1 Concept.** Productized recurring-report automation sprint; sell the outcome before building a general SaaS.

**2 Target market.** Small agencies/operations teams with one repeatable CSV report consuming at least about two staff-hours/week; exclude regulated or highly sensitive data initially.

**3 Demand evidence.** Upwork 2026 contracted earnings support technical/analytics work; current automation requests show buying intent. AgencyAnalytics sells reporting software. This does not validate this exact offer.

**4 Competitive advantage.** Handle the buyer's awkward export and reconciliation accurately; specialize in one workflow that generic dashboards do not solve. Do not sell generic AI summaries.

**5 Exact product / scope.** One agreed report, at most two already-available CSV exports, <=10,000 rows/file, agreed metrics, HTML/CSV output, repeatable script and one revision. No live APIs, scraping, migration or accounting judgement in the pilot.

**6 Pricing (CAD).** First two accepted pilots: 299. Standard after proof: 699. Optional care: 149/month for one defined light workflow; new source formats and large volumes are new quotes.

**7 Setup time.** Offer/demo: one day. Client-specific delivery: target 4–6 hours over 48 hours after scope, payment milestone and approved sample data. Unfamiliar integrations are excluded.

**8 Development process.** Run supplied synthetic demo; agree field mappings and KPI definitions; build fixture tests; adapt parser; reconcile sample and full totals; provide instructions and signed-off output; schedule only after approval.

**9 Tools / accounts.** Local Python and Git; spreadsheet/HTML viewer; marketplace account OR verified Stripe account for independently sourced customers. Use client-owned scheduler/storage initially when appropriate.

**10 Startup budget.** 0–100 for an experiment with existing computer/internet. Registration, insurance and professional review may add costs. Do not buy generic automation subscriptions before a buyer exists.

**11 Acquisition.** Respond manually to explicit reporting/CSV requests; ask permitted warm contacts for introductions; partner with web agencies. Track relevance and permitted communication basis.

**12 First sale.** Send a tailored response to a real buying request with one relevant synthetic example; ask for 10 redacted sample rows; offer a fixed-price milestone and objective acceptance checklist.

**13 Payments.** Upwork-funded milestone for Upwork leads; independently sourced buyers can use Stripe after verification. Do not circumvent platform rules. State cancellation/refund terms and product readiness.

**14 Automated delivery.** Initially manually approved report -> delivery package. Later signed payment webhook -> client intake -> private job queue -> deterministic parser -> checks -> review exceptions -> secure delivery.

**15 Maintenance.** Target <=30 minutes/client/month for standardized care plus monthly dependency checks. Sales, onboarding and development are additional founder time.

**16 Growth.** After three accepted projects, retain reusable non-client-specific code; narrow supported formats; sell care and toolkit. Add agency reseller partners, not unlimited custom integrations.

**17 Financial scenarios.** Isolated active-month illustration: two 699 setups, 13% blended fees/refund allowance, 10 delivery cost each, 0.30 flat each, 110 fixed/acquisition -> about 1,086 before owner pay/tax. Zero sales -> lose 110 plus initial costs. Portfolio model is separate.

**18 Risks.** Scope creep, free/cheap alternatives, bad definitions, leakage of client data, inadequate skills. Never claim delivered production automation from a demo alone.

**19 Kill / pivot gate.** After 30–40 qualified relevant proposals and 10 substantive discussions, no paid pilot -> revise pain/segment once. After two iterations without payment, stop this offer. Decline work that cannot be tested.

**20 Fastest schedule.** Day 1: one-page offer + demo. Day 2: 5–10 high-fit requested-work replies and sample review. Day 3: attempt first paid milestone; deliver only when acceptance conditions are agreed.

Evidence: [U26](https://www.upwork.com/research/in-demand-skills-2026); [UJ](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/automation/); [AA](https://agencyanalytics.com/pricing)

### O02 — Supplier catalog cleanup

**1 Concept.** Supplier-catalog normalization and dry-run import service.

**2 Target market.** Merchants with repeated supplier CSV problems and small ecommerce agencies handling those merchants.

**3 Demand evidence.** Matrixify lists paid plans and has a Shopify listing with user feedback. Existing import tools validate category spending, but strongly constrain a generic import offer.

**4 Competitive advantage.** Sell data cleanup and a reproducible mapping, not software that merely imports a CSV. Specialize by supplier or product vertical.

**5 Exact product / scope.** One supplier, one agreed target import schema, <=1,000 product rows for pilot, SKU/required-field checks, duplicate report and import-ready file. No live product edits included.

**6 Pricing (CAD).** 399 pilot; 499 standardized small import after proof, 999 only for separately scoped complexity. Optional 149/month feed-check care with strict volume/source caps.

**7 Setup time.** Offer/demo 1–2 days; pilot delivery 3–5 hours when source and target schema are known.

**8 Development process.** Obtain authorized redacted CSV; preserve original; profile missing fields; define transformations; create fixtures; export target schema; dry-run in a sandbox; hand over error manifest and rollback instructions.

**9 Tools / accounts.** Python CSV tooling; merchant-owned import tool/account; client-approved test store when needed. No paid public app required.

**10 Startup budget.** 0–100 experiment budget; merchant pays its own importer and store subscription.

**11 Acquisition.** Reply to actual failed-import requests; ask Shopify agencies for overflow cleanup; find multiple merchants of the same willing supplier.

**12 First sale.** Offer a fixed-price cleanup after inspecting a small sample; show exact validation failures, not a fabricated store problem.

**13 Payments.** Marketplace milestone or direct Stripe for independently sourced buyers. Final import/write requires a separate explicit customer approval.

**14 Automated delivery.** Approved file upload -> schema checks -> normalization -> validation manifest -> secure file delivery. Human review remains before destructive changes.

**15 Maintenance.** ~1–2 hours/month per standardized source plus paid new mappings. Schema changes are not unlimited free support.

**16 Growth.** Reuse one supplier mapper across authorized buyers, then add read-only catalog QA. Build a public app only after retained demand.

**17 Financial scenarios.** Three 499 jobs in an active month -> approximately 1,160 operating profit under Unit Economics assumptions. No-sales monthly operating loss about 105 before initial costs.

**18 Risks.** Incorrect variant mapping, overwritten products, image permissions, platform updates and existing low-cost tools.

**19 Kill / pivot gate.** Stop or reprice if small imports exceed four delivery hours repeatedly; do not scale until two sandbox imports reconcile and buyers accept scope.

**20 Fastest schedule.** Day 1: sample and offer. Days 2–3: targeted buyer conversations. Days 3–5: first paid file; no live store writes in the validation experiment.

Evidence: [MX](https://matrixify.app/pricing/); [MR](https://apps.shopify.com/excel-export-import); [UJ](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/automation/)

### O03 — Agency form-path monitoring

**1 Concept.** Agency-managed synthetic form-path monitoring subscription; alert delivery, not unlimited site repair.

**2 Target market.** Small web agencies that maintain at least ten client sites and lack an accountable form-testing process.

**3 Demand evidence.** Checkly sells synthetic monitoring; WordPress maintenance vendors sell recurring care. The demand to validate is for a managed narrow workflow, not basic uptime.

**4 Competitive advantage.** Test the path the agency cares about, direct alerts to the correct owner, keep false alerts low and provide a per-client audit trail.

**5 Exact product / scope.** Two or three agreed checks/site, initially every six hours for browser flows; safe test identifiers and inbox; no real payment charge or user message. Basic HTTP checks may run more often. Define excluded paths and coverage.

**6 Pricing (CAD).** 149/site setup; 79/site/month. Price multiple-page, high-frequency or authenticated flows separately. No perpetual promise of unlimited browser checks.

**7 Setup time.** 3–7 days to a paid beta after written permission and a safe test path.

**8 Development process.** Get approval for target sites and test accounts; define success; build a sandbox test; add explicit cleanup/idempotency; rerun failures to reduce noise; alert owner; record outcomes; reconcile provider usage.

**9 Tools / accounts.** Managed synthetic monitoring such as Checkly or tested Playwright runner; job scheduler, secret storage, notification inbox, Stripe or authorized marketplace subscription.

**10 Startup budget.** 25–150 initial test budget; browser-run costs depend on cadence and must be measured. Cap use before enrolling clients.

**11 Acquisition.** Sell to satisfied reporting/service clients or one agency pilot. A reseller reaching multiple client sites is more efficient than unrelated individual store owners.

**12 First sale.** Offer one paid 30-day pilot with setup and objective coverage; share a failure/success demo on a site you control. Do not probe strangers' forms without permission.

**13 Payments.** Clear recurring billing consent, cancellation and usage terms. Failed payment pauses nonessential checks after notices; no surprise usage overages.

**14 Automated delivery.** Schedule -> browser/HTTP test -> tagged result -> confirmation retry -> owner alert -> daily digest -> monthly report -> usage budget alert. Human approves test designs and security-sensitive changes.

**15 Maintenance.** Target under 30 minutes/site/month for stable paths, plus shared system maintenance. Alerting can run continuously; human repair is not a 24/7 SLA.

**16 Growth.** Add sites through agencies; standardize test templates; bundle but track per-site margin and correlated failures.

**17 Financial scenarios.** Eight sites at 79/month -> about 370 operating profit under the isolated illustration, excluding setup fees. 30 sites at these same costs -> about 1,733 before owner pay/tax, if care remains bounded.

**18 Risks.** False positives, browser changes, missed failures, notification abuse, costly high frequency and implied repair obligations.

**19 Kill / pivot gate.** Require three paying sites and <5% unactionable alerts; reprice if >30 minutes/site/month. Stop adding sites when monitoring cost exceeds budget or incidents cannot be handled safely.

**20 Fastest schedule.** Day 1: permission and scoped pilot. Days 2–4: controlled test and alert demo. Days 5–7: begin paid beta. Do not build a custom monitoring platform first.

Evidence: [CK](https://www.checklyhq.com/pricing/); [WP](https://wpbuffs.com/plans/)

### O04 — Purchase-order PDF to validated CSV

**1 Concept.** Recurring purchase-order/packing-list extraction to a validated CSV review queue.

**2 Target market.** Small distributors and operations offices receiving similar recurring business documents; avoid health records and highly sensitive documents initially.

**3 Demand evidence.** Parseur publishes paid document-extraction plans. This proves category spending, not accuracy for the buyer's files or their willingness to pay you.

**4 Competitive advantage.** Solve one recurring document family with arithmetic and schema checks, preserve provenance and send uncertain values for human review.

**5 Exact product / scope.** One agreed document layout family, <=100 pages/month for starter recurring plan, agreed fields and output schema. No accounting entries, autonomous payments or unsupported handwriting claims.

**6 Pricing (CAD).** 399 paid discovery/pilot for up to 20 representative pages; 699 production setup; optional 199/month up to 100 simple pages, subject to measured provider cost and exception rate.

**7 Setup time.** 2–5 days for representative clean documents; poor scans and many layouts must be separately scoped.

**8 Development process.** Get client permission; assemble representative evaluation set; try embedded PDF text first; use licensed extraction/OCR only when needed; validate required fields, quantities and totals; create exception queue; compare to human-labelled truth; deliver CSV with page/source references.

**9 Tools / accounts.** Text/PDF parser or client-approved Parseur/API account; private storage; deterministic validation; human review. An LLM alone is not the source of numerical truth.

**10 Startup budget.** 0–150 experiment; use free quota or client-paid provider where appropriate. Higher provider subscriptions are not silently absorbed at 199/month.

**11 Acquisition.** Respond to explicit document-to-sheet requests; partner with operators or bookkeepers who will retain responsibility for any accounting.

**12 First sale.** Charge for a narrowly defined pilot with a clear manual-review handoff; use anonymized samples to demonstrate where extraction succeeds and fails.

**13 Payments.** Milestone payment for setup; volume-capped subscription only after evaluation. Refund/rework terms do not guarantee every possible layout.

**14 Automated delivery.** Authorized inbox/upload -> malware/type/size check -> extraction -> validation -> confidence/exception route -> approved CSV delivery -> deletion policy. Nothing posts to a ledger automatically.

**15 Maintenance.** Review outliers; monthly test-set regression; stop automation on layout drift. Target light handling only if exceptions are genuinely rare.

**16 Growth.** Reuse document-family adapters; partner licensing; consider a subscription interface only after repeated paid demand.

**17 Financial scenarios.** Two 699 setups -> about 1,031 active-month operating profit under isolated assumptions. At 199/month, a vendor charging USD49 per 100 pages can materially reduce margin: measure true CAD spend before promising the plan.

**18 Risks.** Incorrect numbers, vendor data retention, confidentiality, scans that require costly OCR, support burden.

**19 Kill / pivot gate.** Do not auto-release results without acceptance-tested checks. Reprice/pivot if >10% of documents require lengthy manual corrections or provider costs break unit margins.

**20 Fastest schedule.** Days 1–2: sample and paid pilot agreement. Days 3–5: labelled test and accepted CSV. Recurring automation only after objective quality sign-off.

Evidence: [PA](https://parseur.com/pricing); [U26](https://www.upwork.com/research/in-demand-skills-2026)

### O05 — Licensed report-automation toolkit

**1 Concept.** Original self-serve toolkit distilled from proven report-automation services; not a second unrelated startup.

**2 Target market.** Freelancers and small agencies with the same report format but insufficient budget for done-for-you setup.

**3 Demand evidence.** Paid reporting software and digital storefronts exist. The exact toolkit has no verified demand; validate through service conversations and paid licenses before expanding.

**4 Competitive advantage.** Documented original code, real tested fixtures, supportable mappings and a transparent demo; not a generic prompt bundle or copied open-source product.

**5 Exact product / scope.** Three original adapters/templates, source code, fixture inputs, expected outputs, install guide, license and supported-version policy. Remove all client identifiers and confidential logic.

**6 Pricing (CAD).** 49 single-user license; 149 team license with explicit seat/project terms. One year of fixes can be offered only if budgeted; no perpetual compatibility or recurring charge without recurring value.

**7 Setup time.** 2–5 days after at least three service deliveries reveal a common reusable pattern; do not spend this time before service validation.

**8 Development process.** Extract only owned reusable code; audit dependency licenses; create clean fixtures; add tests; write license/readme and limitations; package reproducibly; publish honest demo and installation guide.

**9 Tools / accounts.** Local Git/tests, static sales page, eligible digital storefront such as Gumroad/itch or approved Lemon Squeezy product, support inbox.

**10 Startup budget.** 0–100 launch test with existing equipment; no paid ads initially. Marketplace/payment fees remain variable costs.

**11 Acquisition.** Offer to qualified non-buying service leads, get agency partner referrals and post a useful permitted technical demo. Marketplace upload alone is not acquisition.

**12 First sale.** Ask a prospect who declined custom setup whether the tested self-serve version solves their problem; sell a license, not a promise of future broad support.

**13 Payments.** Eligible storefront with receipts/digital fulfillment. Verify taxes, payout eligibility, refund and merchant-of-record treatment; service sales use an appropriate payment channel.

**14 Automated delivery.** Paid receipt -> license record -> versioned ZIP -> installation email -> issue intake -> FAQ lookup -> human review of bugs. Automatic updates only after tests.

**15 Maintenance.** Target <=15 minutes support per sale; weekly triage; periodic version check. Compatibility support must be capped.

**16 Growth.** Team licenses, agency resale/white-label rights by separate agreement, new adapter only after repeated paid requests. Share acquisition and backend with O01.

**17 Financial scenarios.** Ten 49 licenses/month -> about 329 operating profit under isolated assumptions; 100 licenses in the upside model -> about 2,973 after extra overhead. Zero-sales monthly operating loss about 55.

**18 Risks.** Free alternatives, no audience, copied/copyrighted inputs, clients claiming ownership, low-ticket support and account approval.

**19 Kill / pivot gate.** Three paid licenses before more features; <2 sales after 200 qualified visitors -> improve positioning once or stop. Do not build a 100-template catalogue without buyers.

**20 Fastest schedule.** After service proof: day 1 extract/test; day 2 documentation/demo; day 3 list and offer to qualified leads; days 4–5 fix real buyer friction.

Evidence: [AA](https://agencyanalytics.com/pricing); [GM](https://gumroad.com/pricing); [IT](https://itch.io/docs/creators/payments)

## Daily plan — first 30 execution days

**Day 1: Package one offer.** Run synthetic CSV demo; choose one agency-report pain; edit pilot offer and objective acceptance checklist. Gate: A real demo and a 299 CAD offer, not a SaaS build.

**Day 2: Find explicit buying intent.** Review 10 recent relevant requests; select five high-fit buyers; send tailored in-platform responses or permitted introductions. Gate: Five relevant responses; record budget, scope and communication basis.

**Day 3: Seek paid commitment.** Hold up to two scoped discussions; ask for redacted samples; offer a funded milestone; agree safe data handling. Gate: Target one paid pilot; zero sales is a signal to diagnose, not fabricate.

**Day 4: Deliver or refine.** For a buyer: build test fixtures and adapter. Without a buyer: five further high-fit conversations, no new features. Gate: First approved sample OR named objection categories.

**Day 5: Reconcile and hand off.** Test totals, row counts and error handling; deliver with clear limitations and run instructions. Gate: Written acceptance; no production changes without approval.

**Day 6: Offer bounded care.** Ask accepted buyer about repeat runs; quote 149/month only for a genuinely recurring need. Gate: Documented care scope, capped volume, opt-in recurring billing.

**Day 7: Review evidence.** Total proposals, substantive conversations, paid work, delivery hours and objections. Gate: Decision: continue same offer, narrow it, or change the buyer problem.

**Day 8: Standardize the winner.** Keep reusable non-client-specific adapter; document intake and acceptance tests. Gate: One repeatable checklist; no private client data in toolkit.

**Day 9: Sell the second engagement.** Use permissioned proof, not invented testimonial; approach five relevant prospects. Gate: A second paid opportunity or clear pricing/scope feedback.

**Day 10: Measure time saved.** Have buyer compare old and new process; record baseline and measured result. Gate: Evidence-backed benefit; no unsupported ROI promise.

**Day 11: Automate intake.** Use one approved secure intake channel; standardized required fields and file caps. Gate: Fewer missing files/definitions; consent and deletion terms.

**Day 12: Improve error handling.** Add known-schema checks, rejected-row report, failure notification and run IDs. Gate: Reproducible failure test; not silent data corruption.

**Day 13: Clean up scope.** Review revision time; exclude unprofitable integrations; quote new work separately. Gate: Delivery trends toward <=4–6 hours for standard jobs.

**Day 14: Apply first gate.** Review up to three paid deliveries and whether repeat work exists. Gate: No payment after two iterations -> stop/pivot; no second business yet.

**Day 15: Extract the reusable product.** Only with proof, remove private data/code and prepare one self-serve toolkit. Gate: Minimum product solving an already-paid problem.

**Day 16: Validate toolkit demand.** Offer it as a 49 CAD downsell to qualified service prospects. Gate: Target three paid licenses before expansion.

**Day 17: Set up truthful fulfillment.** Versioned ZIP, license, supported versions, cancellation/refund/help details. Gate: No promises of nonexistent features; test a zero-cost internal fulfillment path.

**Day 18: Add measured reporting care.** Run one client-approved scheduled workflow with a manual approval option. Gate: Verified delivery and error alert; rollback and rerun test.

**Day 19: Test the second acquisition route.** Ask one designer/agency for a referral arrangement; disclose any fees. Gate: A real partner conversation, not an assumed distribution agreement.

**Day 20: Reconcile bookkeeping.** Match processor transactions, fees, refunds and payouts; keep tax collected separate. Gate: Actual Dashboard contains only verified actual records.

**Day 21: Review customer retention.** Check care usage, support burden, renewal intent and reasons to cancel. Gate: No fake retention metrics with tiny samples.

**Day 22: Test monitoring need.** Ask existing agency contacts whether safe form-path testing solves an unmet problem. Gate: At least one paid/serious scoped pilot before building.

**Day 23: Run a controlled monitor demo.** On a site you control, test normal/failing states and safe alert routing. Gate: Demonstrated coverage; no probing others without permission.

**Day 24: Sell a bounded monitoring pilot.** Get site-owner authorization, test accounts/inbox and clear no-repair-SLA terms. Gate: Target one paid pilot; otherwise keep selling core offer.

**Day 25: Deliver current obligations.** Fix accepted defects, verify deletion policy and maintain existing reports. Gate: No new launch while delivery backlog is overdue.

**Day 26: Review pricing and contribution.** Calculate customer-level revenue less fees, tools, support and acquisition. Gate: Raise price/narrow scope if contribution or hourly economics fail.

**Day 27: Document delegation.** Write one SOP and acceptance test; do not hire before a stable repeatable task. Gate: A task can be checked objectively before paying a contractor.

**Day 28: Choose reinvestment.** Review channel conversion and contribution; do not buy ads without a proven funnel. Gate: Spend only a capped amount supported by observed demand.

**Day 29: Back up and restore.** Test private backup restoration; rotate exposed credentials; review processor/webhook failures. Gate: An actual restoration and runbook, not just an enabled backup.

**Day 30: Keep, scale or stop.** Review sales, retained care, net profit, owner hours and defects by offer. Gate: Scale only paid, tested demand; pause zero-demand offers and excess support.

## Days 31–90 and months 4–12

Days 31–60: keep one buyer segment and at most three supported source formats. Raise standard price after proof;
sell bounded recurring care to clients who genuinely need it. Build referral/agency distribution.
Only add monitoring after a paid request and safe testing permission. Subcontract one documented, testable task if
its margin supports a fair qualified rate. Do not use contractors to hide unprofitable delivery.

Days 61–90: seek approximately four scoped reporting setups in the modeled month, around five retained care clients,
three monitored sites and five toolkit sales. These are model inputs, not promised targets. Track recurring-only
contribution separately: the base model's month-three profit is mostly helped by new setup work.

Months 4–6: scale the best channel, not every offer. Retest pricing, support minutes, churn and usage costs.
Keep at least two acquisition routes so one marketplace is not the entire business. Ads wait until observed contribution
margin and CAC payback make a controlled test rational; example gate: at least 10 buyers and <3-month contribution payback,
not a supposed universal benchmark.

Months 7–12: deepen the original engine through team licenses, agency partners and new adapters customers actually request.
If quality/backlog deteriorates, pause acquisition before taking more paid obligations. Strong portfolio income requires
continued distribution or a substantial retained base; automated delivery is not automated demand.

Stop-loss rules: no payment after two focused positioning iterations -> pause. Thirty to forty high-fit proposals and
ten actual buyer discussions without a sale call for a different problem or segment, not a prettier website.
An inability to safely deliver a task is a reason to narrow it or acquire competence before selling.
Do not keep an unvalidated side project running because infrastructure is interesting.

## Automation architecture

Start with one local deterministic workflow, not a multi-agent platform. Once two clients retain the same workflow,
use: approved intake -> private tenant storage -> bounded job queue -> schema checks -> deterministic transformation
-> reconciliation tests -> human exception review -> secure report delivery -> immutable run log -> billing/metrics ledger.

Payment path: hosted checkout or funded marketplace milestone -> signed, idempotent payment event ->
entitlement/job record. Treat refunds, cancellations, duplicate/out-of-order events and failed payments explicitly.
Do not count unfulfilled deposits as earned revenue or processor payouts as new revenue.

Use Python for parsing/math, simple JavaScript only for interfaces, Git for version control and tests, and
a managed scheduler/queue for customer-critical timing. GitHub Actions can run CI and noncritical regression tests;
scheduled runs are not an exact delivery-time guarantee. A small managed runner may be safer than a home computer.
A valid implementation must fit the modeled variable costs and include secrets storage and backup restoration.

An LLM may draft explanations, code and help responses, but numbers must come from tested calculations.
Untrusted uploaded text is data, never instructions granting tools access. Do not put unrestricted email,
billing, production-write or refund tools behind customer-provided prompts.

Shared modules: customer ID, product entitlement, schema adapter, delivery engine, license/version registry,
run IDs, support triage, billing ledger and source/consent register. Use separate tenant data and least-privilege
credentials. A second product should reuse tested infrastructure and the SAME buyer channel, not assume free demand.

Responsible automation boundaries: humans approve scope, rights and data-processing terms, KPI definitions,
new integrations, client-facing exceptions, DNS/production changes, refunds, destructive writes and legal/tax decisions.
Support automation classifies and drafts; it does not invent answers or promise repairs. Marketing automation organizes
permitted leads, records consent and schedules approved content; it does not scrape and spam prospects.

Reliability baseline: input size/type limits, private storage, encryption in transit/at rest, short retention,
signed download links, server-side authorization, safe retries, idempotency keys, dead-letter queue, spend caps,
customer-visible job status, incident runbook, dependency tests and restore-tested backups.

Sources for implementation constraints: Stripe webhooks (WH), GitHub schedules (GH), n8n license (N8L), cloud pricing (CW),
OpenAI billing (OA), and Canadian privacy obligations (PR). AI API use is billed separately from a ChatGPT subscription.
n8n's licensing needs review before embedding or selling hosted access; internal automation is not the same business model
as selling someone else's platform.

## Central management system

The workbook's Actuals, Operations and Actual Dashboard sheets are a functional empty tracker.
They show per-offer revenue, expenses/refunds, operating profit, recurring revenue, customer movement, conversion,
automation fraction, failures, run success, owner hours, profitable offers and reinvestment prompts.
Import only deduplicated verified records. No live integrations or actual balances are present.

At first, import weekly CSV exports manually. After stable revenue, connect permitted payment webhooks/provider APIs,
job-run events and support/time logs through a nightly ETL job. Retain raw event IDs and reconcile to the provider monthly.
Do not derive net revenue by adding Stripe bank payouts to sales; those payouts settle previous transactions.
A shared accounting ledger is the source of monetary truth; analytics dashboards are views.

Daily 10-minute review is a target only for stable systems: urgent failures, client delivery and payment exceptions.
Weekly 45-minute management review: reconciled profit, retention, contribution per owner hour, defects,
qualified conversion and CAC payback. That review time is NOT the whole sales/maintenance workload.
Only reinvest where observed customer acquisition contribution and retention justify the spend.

## Interpreting the financial model

All figures are CAD. Operating profit subtracts fees, software/hosting, acquisition spending, delivery costs,
refund and FX/payment-risk reserves, contractors and initial setup spending. Owner wages and income taxes are NOT
subtracted there; the model separately values owner time at CAD30/hour. Sales taxes collected are not revenue.

The base scenario is a conditional operating plan, not a statistically estimated outcome. It assumes:
two 299 reporting pilots in month one, then three/four/five 699 setups as shown; 60% attachment to 149 care;
4% monthly care/monitor churn; 79 monitoring subscriptions plus 149 setups; and 49 toolkit licenses.
Use average opening/closing retained clients for approximate earned subscription revenue, and closing clients for exit MRR.
Fractional expected customer counts are an approximation, not literally sellable fractions of customers.

Platform/service fee allowance: half service revenue at a 15% marketplace stress rate, half at a 4% direct
blended percentage plus CAD0.30/direct transaction. Toolkit: 15% plus CAD0.75/transaction. These are deliberately
simplified allowances, not exact tariff quotes. Add 3% refunds/rework and 2% FX/payment-risk reserves. Actual provider
rules, currencies, card origin, tax on fees and minimum subscriptions may require higher costs.

Budget includes no existing device/internet replacement or opportunity cost of already paid subscriptions.
Client-specific expensive subscriptions must be priced in or customer-funded. Business registration, insurance,
professional services, card disputes and taxes must be replaced by actual records before trusting take-home cash.
A reserve is not evidence of a known cost or tax rate.

The optimistic case requires broader distribution and significant paid help. At month 12 it assumes 16 new
reporting setups, about 80 retained care clients, about 158 monitored sites and 300 toolkit licenses that month.
It also budgets 360 contractor hours, 4,200 acquisition spend and 1,800 fixed/admin costs that month.
It is not a solo, five-hours-a-week passive business.

The no-traction scenario stops after a small test rather than burning cash all year. A completely zero-sales
outcome is also possible. Base and optimistic numbers are not probabilities, guarantees or validated earnings.

Canadian implementation: confirm provincial registration and privacy rules, record business income/expenses,
and review GST/HST small-supplier registration rules based on taxable supplies, not profit. Exports and merchant-of-record
sales do not remove the need to determine the seller's own Canadian obligations. The 30% tax cash reserve is a placeholder,
not an estimate of tax payable. See CRA (TX), CRTC (CR) and OPC (PR) primary sources; obtain situation-specific advice.

Distribution rules: comply with CASL and each platform. A published business email is not blanket permission.
Prioritize replies to explicit requests, permitted introductions and relevant approved community posts.
Never use fake reviews, copied art, franchise characters, cloned voices, unauthorized scraped datasets or misleading results.
For original game assets, independently verify ownership and license compatibility before selling.
