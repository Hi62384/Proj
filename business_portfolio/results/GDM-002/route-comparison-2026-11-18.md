# GDM-002 alternative-route experiment — 2026-11-18

The untested hypothesis was that time-dependent rerouting through alternate scheduled services might differentiate the proposed coordinator from a small DIY solution. Two original local feasibility planners were built: a bounded earliest-arrival graph search and a separate bounded simple-path enumeration. Neither allocates seats or integrates into the journey state machine. The candidate accepts at most 100 stops and 500 services; the DIY enumerator intentionally caps its comparison at 10 stops, 30 services and 100,000 search expansions. These limits and different algorithms prevent treating the small test as a scalability or engineering-effort comparison.

`test_route_planner.gd` passed on Godot 4.5.1 for an earliest transfer, service disablement and alternate route, unreachable destination, missed departure, invalid schedule, duplicate ID, deterministic local tie handling and a zero-leg journey. The first run failed to compile because the function lacked an explicit final return; this was corrected. `research/test_route_comparison.gd` then checked 500 seeded five-stop graphs. Both planners agreed on reachability and earliest arrival and returned valid itineraries in every case; eight cases selected different valid equal-arrival routes. The candidate prefers earliest arrival at each intermediate stop and uses a stable service-ID key for equal intermediate arrivals; the enumerator selects the lexicographically smaller full itinerary among equal final arrivals. The first comparison incorrectly required identical routes and reported two failures in 100 cases; the corrected comparison validates both itineraries and compares their arrival time, while explicitly counting route differences. This distinction must be resolved in an eventual public contract if one is created.

`research/test_route_ledger.gd` passed a manually orchestrated single-passenger scenario using each planner: after a connection is disabled, the old reservation is canceled, its late boarding attempt fails, and an alternate service is boarded and later vacated with no remaining seat claims. This is not an automatic scheduler, real movement integration, concurrent capacity-aware route planning, or a complete disruption workflow. All three commands exited 0 on the passing runs and emitted the sandbox root-certificate warning.

The candidate first passed after approximately 77 seconds; the later DIY implementation and its first comparison took approximately 49 seconds before the subsequent tie-policy investigation. The selected tests do not demonstrate a candidate advantage: both approaches found equivalent earliest arrival times on the bounded cases, and the manually orchestrated ledger scenario worked with both. The technical gate remains unpassed and packaging remains no-go. A stronger decision would require an explicit tie contract, automated integration of routing with a genuine multi-agent journey coordinator, equivalent fresh-controller setup and QA evidence, and bounded performance measurements. No spending, publication, customer contact or sales occurred.

A subsequent bounded experiment added `automatic_reroute_fixture.gd`, a fixed three-service, two-leg coordinator shared by both planners. With 20 synthetic passengers, disabling the second service while three passengers wait automatically cancels those queue entries and selects the alternate service. `test_automatic_reroute.gd` passed for both route planners: each produced the same 144-event trace, completed all 20 journeys, stayed within seat capacity, left no ghost seats and resumed identically from a trusted local disk snapshot. It also checked rejection of duplicate-ownership and wrong-stop snapshots, explicit no-route outcomes when both onward services are disabled, and refusal to disable a service that currently has riders without changing state. The fixture uses the candidate's graph validator and the same seat ledger and coordinator for both planner runs, so it is not an independent end-to-end DIY implementation or an integration-effort comparison. Boarding is immediate and no external controller or real physics is involved. It refuses an in-transit service disable rather than implementing breakdown rescue.

During this work, side effects were moved out of `assert` expressions so they are not dependent on debug assertions being enabled. The 500-graph comparison was also strengthened to reverse the service input order for each graph; candidate results remained deterministic. The same eight equal-arrival itinerary differences remain between planners. Six affected Godot scripts passed; the root-certificate warning remained. The wall interval from 20:39:42 to 20:41:44 UTC was approximately 2 minutes 2 seconds, including coding, tests and debugging. It is not a focused-human effort measure and does not resolve the unknown prior use of the six-hour cap. The local technical gate remains unpassed and packaging remains no-go.

A further fixed-scenario comparison extended the automatic coordinator to handle a disruption while two passengers are in transit. The ledger-based version retains their seats as stranded, saves/restores that state, rejects stale, duplicate and unsupported-stop rescue acknowledgments, then replans from the supported origin stop. Twenty synthetic passengers completed the same 150-event trace under either route planner. A separately written FIFO coordinator and enumerating planner then produced the same trace and outcomes. Its initial restore accepted a wrong-stop snapshot that the ledger fixture rejected; after additional validation, the independent version rejected that snapshot and matched the trace as well. Both versions were written by the same AI with knowledge of the scenario, and their save validators are not complete or independently security-audited.

The two coordinators were also wired to 20 `CharacterBody3D` bodies with native NavigationAgent3D and then FlowAI. In each test two B-service riders were physically between stops when interrupted, the schedule was frozen while the shared controller navigated them back to the hub, and all 20 arrived after replanning. Each implementation produced the same 150-event trace in 71 schedule ticks. The bodies have no collision obstacles or physical vehicles in this scenario; boarding is immediate, controller behavior is shared, and there is no automated full-controller disk restore in this particular disruption test. FlowAI again reported ObjectDB and three resource warnings on shutdown. Nine affected Godot scripts passed in a later batch, with the root-certificate warning.

`test_capacity_gap.gd` reproduces a missed-capacity case in the two default coordinators: after the B vehicle fills, `npc02` remains queued and arrives at tick 10 even though replanning at tick 4 identifies C arriving at tick 7. The opt-in capacity-replan mode in each coordinator revisits the route after a full departure, transfers waiting passengers after all current-tick departures close, and on this fixture arrives at tick 7. `test_capacity_replan.gd` and `research/test_capacity_replan_comparison.gd` passed the scenario, trusted snapshot/replay, same traces between implementations, unsupported snapshot-version rejection, and a simultaneous-departure check. The old default remains unchanged so the earlier trace and gap remain reproducible. Candidate automatic-fixture snapshots are now version 3; the validated DIY snapshots are version 2. No migration is supplied.

This is a fixed-graph heuristic, not a reservation-aware earliest-arrival guarantee: the planner ignores future seat claims and the heuristic has not been shown free of oscillation, unfairness, starvation or excessive replanning on general graphs. It has not been tested with the 20 real-navigation controllers, vehicle physics or arbitrary NPC movement. Both implementations were written by the same AI after seeing the same counterexample, and their matching results do not demonstrate a competitive advantage. The technical gate remains unpassed and the project stays paused. A later adversarial capacity check tested the opt-in missed-departure replan against staying in the current queue. In 100 seeded synthetic fixed-topology schedules, all 20 passengers completed within the test window. The simple replan produced at least one later-arriving passenger in 44 cases and at least one earlier-arriving passenger in 66 cases; those counts can overlap. One reproducible case used B `(period 7, offset 3, duration 4, capacity 3)` and C `(period 6, offset 1, duration 5, capacity 2)`: `npc08` arrived at tick 14 when staying and tick 18 with the replan. `research/test_capacity_counterexample_replay.gd` reproduced the same delayed result in the separately written DIY coordinator. This is a seeded synthetic sample, not a representative performance or market estimate.

A further opt-in forecast estimated the currently visible queue and onboard releases for direct hub-to-end services before switching. The first assertion expecting improvement failed; `research/test_queue_forecast.gd` now explicitly reproduces that failure, so its PASS means the counterexample was reproduced, not that the policy is safe. In the same 100-case sample, the forecast produced at least one later passenger in 44 cases and at least one earlier passenger in 80 cases; again, those categories overlap. All runs completed within the test window. A separate fixed-fixture test confirmed trusted snapshot/replay for the forecast flag. Candidate snapshot version is now 4 and earlier fixture versions are rejected without migration.

Both replan modes are experimental and disabled by default. A further experimental guarded replan simulates the known passengers' fixed future before accepting a switch, and accepts only when that simulation predicts at least one earlier arrival and none later. It does not predict new requests, disruptions or further replans, and caps its internal trial at 20 passengers and 512 ticks. In 30 seeded static schedules, `research/test_guarded_sample.gd` observed no later passenger and at least one earlier passenger in 21 cases; 83 switches occurred. A separately written FIFO version matched the guarded traces in those 30 cases (`research/test_guarded_diy.gd`). Both versions were authored by the same AI for the same experiment, so this is not independent integration-effort evidence.

The static condition is not a guarantee under changing inputs. In 100 seeded cases with service B disabled at tick 6 and immediate fixture-level rescue, `research/test_guarded_disruption.gd` found at least one later passenger in 4 cases and at least one earlier passenger in 13 cases; categories may overlap and none were incomplete within the test window. The deterministic replay showed `npc01` arriving at tick 8 without switching and tick 12 with the guard; the DIY implementation matched the guarded trace. `research/test_guarded_identical_prefix.gd` verified identical histories before the disruption: without the disruption this passenger arrived at ticks 8/7 for stay/guard, while with it the outcome was 8/12. These samples are synthetic and do not establish general rates or a formal impossibility theorem.

The guard is experimental and disabled by default. Its lookahead assumes no unknown external changes and has no real-controller, scalability, fairness or production-performance proof.
