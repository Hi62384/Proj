# Reconciled research: fast cash flow vs. low-workload digital products

**Research date:** 13 October 2026 · **Repo baseline:** `86d724f58d3647ab2822d7904128be882cba8b29` · **Currency:** CAD.

## What changed and what did not

This is an **additive integration**, not a replacement of the repository's independently audited 54-opportunity study. The original `opportunities.json`, 89-source `sources.json`, `financial_model.mjs`, `financial_summary.json`, `Automated_Online_Income_Portfolio.html`, and `Portfolio_Dashboard.html` are unchanged. The fast-cash 60-opportunity research, source registry, standalone workbook, complete playbook, and templates are imported into `research_merge/`.

The searchable `Combined_Online_Income_Research.html` presents **114 source opportunity records**, **18 conservatively marked equivalent pairs**, **31 adjacent relationship pairs**, and **96 distinct conceptual families** after joining only explicit equivalent pairs. The 96 number is a transparent clustering choice, **not** a claim that every remaining offer has independent validated demand. Relationship mapping is in `overlap_crosswalk.csv`.

**No purchases, customers, interviews, website deployments, account access, advertising, outreach or payment setup were performed.** The source lists are not a prospect database. Pricing and category signals establish competition and spending in a category, not demand for a particular seller's product.

The repo's high original ranking of assets does not demonstrate immediate buyer availability. Conversely, the fast-cash study's high rank for reporting services depends on being able to find and convert customers, and on competent scope-bounded delivery. These are falsifiable assumptions, not observed facts.

## Prioritized experiment queue

1. **Service cash-flow test:** $299 fixed-scope recurring-report automation, with a synthetic demonstration, two permitted exports maximum and objective acceptance. Evaluate five *relevant explicit buyer requests*; use platform-approved funded milestones. Do not make claims about prior clients.
2. **Asset craft counter-test (optional, not simultaneous if time is constrained):** if original commercial-quality assets already come quickly, cap five assets at 12h, test clean Godot imports and ask five relevant game developers to compare a real sampler against free substitutes. Two genuine paid purchases justify extending the pack. Avoid third-party fictional/franchise art unless rights are secured.
3. **Offline localization QA tool:** prove a narrow local deterministic parser and buyer willingness to pay before building a user interface or promising engine-wide compatibility. Strong substitutes such as Weblate must be explicitly addressed.
4. **Follow validated buyer needs:** recurring reporting care, a licensed reporting toolkit, agency form monitoring, catalog CSV cleanup, document extraction, or a specific reusable API. No subscription without clear recurring value and realistic support economics.
5. **Do not prioritize:** generic affiliate sites, open-ended AI assistants, job boards, broad print-on-demand, or multi-tenant SaaS without customer access. They remain in the database for later validation.

## First 72 hours — one primary experiment

**Day 1:** Run the provided synthetic reporting example and reproduce its tests; verify what it can and cannot do. Define the target buyer, CAD299 pilot scope, output, acceptance criteria, privacy limitations, one revision and refund/repair remedy. Spend no acquisition money without a measurable channel.

**Day 2:** Search existing explicit requests on platforms you are allowed to use and/or contact warm referrals with permission. Select five genuinely relevant buyers. Answer with a truthful demonstration and request ten redacted sample rows. Track permission, platform terms, rejection reasons and time spent. Do not send bulk unsolicited commercial messages.

**Day 3:** Attempt to secure one paid milestone. If paid, adapt the single agreed workflow, verify math against the source, deliver instructions and obtain acceptance. If not paid, classify objections and check channel fit before adding features. A job posting, positive reply or download is not a paid order.

## Days 4–30

Days 4–7: resolve delivery defects or interview/reply to another controlled batch of qualified buyers. Record the exact path from visible request → relevant conversation → proposal → funded order → acceptance → settlement. Do not count money waiting for payout as spendable.

Days 8–14: aim for a second independent paid delivery, reduce rework, set an intake checklist, add regression fixtures and measure all human time. Discuss maintenance **only with customers who need actual recurring runs**. If asset test offers stronger evidence of paid demand than service outreach, stop service acquisition rather than running two full operations.

Days 15–30: improve the winning offer's acquisition and price; create a toolkit *only* from reusable, properly licensed implementation code and without any client information. Launch a second stream only if it neither harms client commitments nor exceeds a defined weekly work cap.

## Days 31–90 and beyond

Standardize delivery and support. Separate the immediate cash-flow business from the product/software experiment, sharing tests, documentation, metrics, privacy controls, billing events and an exception dashboard. Use actual conversion, contribution per sale, owner hours, churn and unreserved settled cash to replace model assumptions. Consider agency monitoring only after a paid consented pilot; catalog/data SaaS only after distinct paid pilots with repeat usage. Prefer profit per owner hour over adding more nominal streams.

Its 24-month base remains negative in cumulative economic profit. Its optimistic month-12 at CAD500 is approximately CAD1,776 operating profit but requires **37.6 owner hours**. The faster service plan assumes four new standardized setups by month 3, subsequent retained care clients, monitoring subscriptions, and a toolkit sales channel. **Do not add** both models' revenue or profit, assume the same human hours are available to each, translate conditional run rates into probability-weighted forecasts, or assume 90-day recurring revenue equals passive income.

### Decision gates

- **No promise of first-week income.** Seek payment, not vanity signals. If 30–40 *qualified, permitted* service proposals with at least ten substantive discussions produce no order, revise buyer/problem/pricing rather than constructing a SaaS.
- **Digital product craft:** stop the asset line if a five-piece competitive demonstration needs more than 12 hours or users consistently choose free substitutes. Limit pre-sale production spend.
- **Software:** require multiple real paid trials and repeat use before expanding features; keep a free sample genuinely functional, with no manipulative trial or fabricated reviews.
- **Recurring service:** cap scope, job frequency, authorized data access and response obligations; human review for financial figures and extracted documents.
- **Paid acquisition:** spend only after measured contribution covers realistic CAC; recheck payment/provider and Canadian tax status before launch.
